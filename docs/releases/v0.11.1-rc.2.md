ProxCloud v0.11.1-rc.2 is the second release candidate of `v0.11.1`, published as a prerelease. `v0.11.1` completes layer 4 of the load balancer: UDP, TLS termination with certificates, and the layer 4 options, for a tenant's own balancers and for the Services of a managed Kubernetes cluster. The lab drill of [v0.11.1-rc.1](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.1-rc.1) found one defect that must not reach the release, and smaller ones. This candidate holds their fixes, so that the drill can go on. None of these fixes has run on a lab yet. It is not for production. `v0.11.0` stays the current stable release and `latest`.

The BOM (bill of materials) is the list of exact versions a managed-services platform runs. The BOM of this candidate is `0.11.1-rc.2`. Its pins are those of `0.11.1-rc.1`. Against `0.11.0` it moves one thing: the cluster chart for new tenant clusters, `proxmox-kamaji-cluster` `0.1.17` → `0.1.19`.

## Highlights

- **The defect that stopped rc.1 is fixed.** With rc.1, a pair that served a UDP listener read as degraded, and Services made afterwards never showed their address. See "Fixed since rc.1".
- **Three more fixes a user can see.** The first appliance of a pair serves alone. A damaged certificate file is noticed and healed. A project sees which public addresses its quota counts.
- **Five listener protocols.** A listener is `tcp`, `udp`, `tls`, `http` or `https`. `v0.11.0` served `tcp` and `http` and refused the rest.
- **UDP, on a pair.** A highly available load balancer takes UDP listeners. The kernel's IPVS (LVS) balances the datagrams on the appliance that holds the address, next to HAProxy. A TCP and a UDP listener may share a port number. A balancer on one VM refuses UDP.
- **TLS ends on the balancer.** A project uploads certificates with their keys. A `tls` listener passes TCP to the servers, an `https` listener passes HTTP. One listener presents up to ten certificates and picks by the server name the client asks for (SNI). TLS 1.2 and 1.3 only, with a fixed list of suites.
- **The layer 4 options.** Session persistence by source address, PROXY protocol version 1 or 2, health checks of type `tcp`, `http`, `https`, `tls-hello` and `none` with their own interval, timeout, rise and fall, three timeouts, and a connection limit per listener.
- **Kubernetes Services get the same.** A Service of a managed cluster can have UDP ports, TCP and UDP on one address, session affinity, TLS from a `kubernetes.io/tls` Secret, and a set of `proxcloud.io/lb-*` annotations. A Service can also leave its public address to its cluster and take it again later.
- **The controller watches.** With cluster chart `0.1.19` a change of a Service or a node is sent to the portal about a second after it happens. Before, the controller listed the cluster every 10 s.
- **An existing cluster gets none of this by the portal's upgrade.** It keeps the controller it was made with until it is moved to chart `0.1.19`. Until then its UDP Services stay refused, so nothing gets an address and a bill without its owner's action. See step 5 of "Upgrade".
- **From `v0.11.0`, every load balancer is reloaded once after the upgrade.** The HAProxy config changed for every balancer, so each one is pushed once, the standby first. See step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **From rc.1, only keepalived on a pair is reloaded.** The HAProxy config is the same to the byte. A single balancer gets nothing. See step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **No request is lost at a reload or a node change any more** (#430). `v0.11.0` listed this as a known issue. On the lab, under rc.1, a client loop counted 31365 requests and 0 failed across the upgrade, three pushes and eight changes of the listener list. A node change under load was not run there.
- **Only `proxcloud.io/lb-*` annotations are read.** The product defines its own names. A manifest written for another cloud's controller must be renamed first: see Known issues.
- **What ran on a lab ran on rc.1.** The upgrade from `v0.11.0` and TLS on a single balancer. Nothing of this candidate's own fixes has run on a lab.

## Fixed since rc.1

Three pull requests, all found by the lab drill of rc.1. The four fixes a user can see:

- **A pair with a UDP listener read as degraded, and its new Services never showed their address** (#447). This was the must-fix of the drill. With rc.1, once a pair had a UDP listener the portal could no longer tell which appliance held the address. The balancer read `DEGRADED` "Neither appliance holds the address yet". Every Service of the cluster made or changed afterwards stayed pending in its status, although it answered. The health view listed no member, and a push could reach the appliance that holds the address before the standby. The appliances themselves served throughout. A pair that reads degraded by this becomes `ACTIVE` at the first pass after the upgrade, with nothing to do.
- **The first appliance of a pair serves alone** (#446). A cluster's pair is built one appliance at a time. Until the second existed, the first could not bind its addresses: the Services showed an address that answered nobody. On the lab, under `v0.11.0`, the first answer came 9 min 14 s after the Services were made and 6 min 50 s after they had shown their address. Where no node has room for the second appliance it would not come. Now an appliance without a peer holds the addresses and serves TCP, TLS and UDP. The balancer reads `DEGRADED` with no standby until the second joins, and no address moves when it does. The same holds for a tenant's pair left with one appliance by a replace.
- **A damaged certificate file is noticed and healed** (#448). With rc.1, a certificate file truncated or deleted on an appliance went unnoticed while HAProxy ran. HAProxy's next start then refused the whole config, and the plain listeners went down with the TLS ones. Now every pass checks the certificate files a config names: present, the right bytes, and root's alone. A file that fails is written again in that pass, with one reload and no closed connection.
- **A project sees which public addresses its quota counts** (#446). A project at its limit read "Quota exceeded for floatingIps: 5 in use" and could not tell which five. `GET /api/v1/project/{id}/quota-usage` now answers the count in use and one row per address with what holds it. The tenant dashboard shows a "Public addresses" tile with the list.

Smaller fixes of the same drill:

- **A change on a balancer with TLS writes only the certificate files that differ** (#448). With rc.1 every change wrote every file again: a change answered after 8.8 s with two certificates and after 13.5 s with eleven on the lab. The time saved was not measured.
- **Both consoles name the two cases in which a cluster's update changes a served Service** (#447): a `proxcloud.io/lb-internal` that is not false, and a `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` that names no usable Secret. With rc.1 they said that no served Service changes.
- **One `LoadBalancerIPIgnored` Warning** for a Service that names an address its cluster does not keep (#447). With rc.1 it got two, with two texts.
- **The log line "keepalived config is not the one its row records" means a hand edit or a lost file** (#447). With rc.1 it was written at every planned change: 16 lines in 18 minutes on the lab.
- **keepalived's track script asks HAProxy up to four times, 0.2 s apart** (#447). With rc.1 one reload of HAProxy could cost the appliance that holds the address a failed run, and two failed runs in a row move the addresses.
- **Every file of a push is written beside its place and moved in by a rename** (#447). With rc.1 the track script could be read empty for a moment during a push.
- **Guides corrected** (#448). Only the last `X-Forwarded-For` value is the balancer's. With the PROXY protocol on, a health check on the member's own port carries the header too. A certificate file damaged by hand does not give a refused config.

## Upgrade

Use a lab or another non-production site. Production sites should stay on `v0.11.0` and wait for `v0.11.1`. These steps upgrade from `v0.11.0` or from `v0.11.1-rc.1`. From an older release, read the [notes of v0.11.0](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.0) first: their steps and cautions still apply. The figures below were measured on one lab under rc.1. Nothing was measured on this candidate.

1. **Before the upgrade, look at three things.**

   - **The platform's BOM.** The move is planned from the BOMs `0.10.1`, `0.10.2`, `0.11.0`, their candidates and `0.11.1-rc.1`. A platform on an older BOM (`0.8.0` to `0.10.0` and their candidates, all on Kubernetes 1.35.10) is refused by this build. Move it to `0.10.1` first with the `v0.10.1` build, both parts, as the [notes of v0.10.1](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.10.1) describe.
   - **Services that carry `proxcloud.io/lb-internal`.** `v0.11.0` did not read this annotation. From this release a Service gets a public address only when the annotation is absent or false. A Service with `"true"`, `"yes"` or an empty value loses its address when its cluster gets the new controller. Set it to `"false"` or remove it where the address must stay. On the lab, under rc.1, two such Services lost their address within half a minute of their cluster's update.
   - **A time for the pushes of step 2.**

2. **Upgrade the portal.** On the Docker Compose host, check out the tag and run the upgrade. The script takes its backup first. `compose.yaml` did not change since `v0.11.0`. See ["Upgrade" in the Compose guide](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.2/deploy/compose/README.md#upgrade).

   ```sh
   git fetch --tags && git checkout v0.11.1-rc.2
   ./proxcloud-up.sh upgrade v0.11.1-rc.2
   ```

   **From `v0.11.0`: every load balancer is pushed once.**

   - **Why.** The config the platform renders for an existing balancer is no longer the one `v0.11.0` rendered: it names a state file, tries a refused connect on another member, waits 2 s for a connect where it waited 5 s, and names its backends by pool. The files of a pair's keepalived changed too. So each balancer's recorded hash differs, and its next pass pushes it.
   - **What a push is.** One HAProxy reload per appliance, and one keepalived reload per appliance of a pair. A reload is not a restart: open connections are kept.
   - **The standby first.** A pair's standby gets the config before the appliance that holds the address. A config that HAProxy on the standby does not take is not sent to the other one. On each appliance the apply checks the file, reloads, proves that HAProxy listens on every address of it, and puts the old file back otherwise.
   - **What it costs once.** This reload is the last one that starts every member as up. A balancer with a dead member, or a Service with `externalTrafficPolicy: Local`, sees the known 2 to 4 seconds of lost requests once more.
   - **Until its push**, the health view of a balancer lists its members as `NOT LOADED`. The listeners are not affected.
   - **A balancer without a UDP or TLS listener gets no new package and one new file**, the state file.
   - **This ran on the lab under rc.1**, as described: see "Verified on a lab". This candidate renders the same HAProxy config as rc.1.

   **From `v0.11.1-rc.1`: a pair gets keepalived's files once more, and nothing else is pushed.**

   - **Why.** Two files of a pair changed since rc.1: keepalived's track script and its notify script. `keepalived.conf` is the same to the byte.
   - **What happens.** The first pass writes keepalived's five files to each appliance of a pair and reloads keepalived once per appliance, the standby first. A reload keeps keepalived's state.
   - **HAProxy is not touched.** `haproxy.cfg` is the same to the byte as under rc.1, so it is not pushed and HAProxy is not reloaded. The files of a pair's UDP listeners are the same too.
   - **A single balancer gets nothing.** It runs no keepalived.
   - **A pair degraded by the rc.1 defect becomes `ACTIVE` at that first pass.** Its cluster's pending Services become ready, and the controller writes their address into their status at its next call after the pass.
   - **This did not run on a lab.** It was read from the code and is held by tests. The same keepalived reload at the upgrade to rc.1 made no VRRP transition on the lab.

   **From either one:**

   - **When.** At the first services sync after the upgrade. For a cluster's balancer, at its controller's next call. **Run a services sync right after the upgrade**, at a time of your choosing, on a site without a scheduler. On the lab, under rc.1, the cluster's pair was pushed within a minute of the upgrade and the single balancer by the first services sync.
   - **A pair that has one appliance at the upgrade** gets keepalived as its own master and serves alone from then on.
   - **Certificate files are now checked at each pass.** A balancer with a TLS listener is asked for the state of its certificate files with the state read of every pass. A file that is whole causes no push. A balancer without a TLS listener is asked nothing more.

3. **Read the plan.** Open System → Managed services and read the plan in step 4. It refuses nothing. From the BOM `0.11.0` (and from `0.11.0-rc.1` and `0.11.0-rc.2`) it lists two changes:

   | Change | From → to | Class | Stage | Wave |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | `release` | `0.11.0` → `0.11.1-rc.2` | record only | none | |
   | `tenantCharts.kubernetes` | `proxmox-kamaji-cluster` `0.1.17` → `0.1.19` | record only | none | |

   From the BOM `0.11.1-rc.1` it lists one:

   | Change | From → to | Class | Stage | Wave |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | `release` | `0.11.1-rc.1` → `0.11.1-rc.2` | record only | none | |

4. **Run the platform upgrade.** Type `upgrade <installation name> to 0.11.1-rc.2`. There is one action, with no parts, and no seed backup is asked for.

   - **What it does.** It records the new release and writes chart `0.1.19` on the Kubernetes provider. Clusters created after it get the new chart and the controller that watches.
   - **What it does not do.** It replaces no machine, syncs nothing and writes nothing on Proxmox. The platform cluster stays on chart `0.1.16`. No tenant cluster changes chart, and no balancer is touched by this run.
   - **No sandbox.** The BOM holds no sandbox component, so the run installs none.
   - **On the lab, under rc.1,** the run from `0.11.0` took 44 s, and every running VM kept its QEMU process.

5. **Move the clusters that should get the new features.** A cluster made before this release keeps its controller. Its UDP Services stay `UDPNotSupported`, and no annotation but `proxcloud.io/lb-proxy-protocol: "true"` is read. Both consoles say which controller a cluster has. Any one of these moves a cluster to chart `0.1.19` and to the portal's controller image:

   - the tenant's platform update on the cluster's page;
   - the operator's **Bump** of the chart version;
   - the operator's **Enable load balancer controller**, run again (it also replaces the controller's credential).

   The move rolls no machine and changes no balancer config. After it, a Service with a UDP port that stood refused gets a public address, which is billed and counts in the floating IP quota. A cluster that was moved under rc.1 needs no second move. See ["UDP, TLS and the controller of an earlier release"](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.2/docs/managed-k8s.md#9-how-a-cluster-lands-on-proxmox).

6. **Give the site what UDP and TLS need.** Skip this step and nothing breaks.

   - **UDP needs a pair.** A balancer made with `ha: false`, or made before `v0.11.0`, refuses a UDP listener.
   - **UDP needs `nftables` on the appliance.** The first UDP listener installs that one package from apt where the image lacks it. A site with no egress bakes it into `config.lbImage`.
   - **UDP members must answer a ping from the appliance.** The default health check sends a datagram and a ping. A security group that admits the UDP port but no ICMP takes the member out. Admit ICMP from the load balancer, or choose a check with a required reply, a TCP check or an HTTP check.
   - **TLS needs the portal's encryption key.** On a portal whose `ENCRYPTION_KEY` is missing or shorter than 32 characters, a certificate upload answers `503` and nothing is stored.
   - **Protect `backups/`.** The upgrade's backup writes the database dump and a copy of `.env` side by side. From this release that directory holds what opens every tenant's private key. See Known issues.

7. **If you roll the portal back to `v0.11.0`, read this first.**

   - **A Service that ends TLS with a Secret loses its address.** The old portal ends no TLS, so the controller does not send it such a Service. Take `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` off the Services whose address must stay, before the rollback.
   - **Do not move a cluster's chart while the portal is rolled back.** Chart `0.1.19` starts the controller with `--watch=true`, which the image of `v0.11.0` does not know. See Known issues.
   - **A balancer with a UDP or TLS listener is not served by `v0.11.0`.** Remove such listeners first.
   - **Release the kept addresses first.** `v0.11.0` does not know a cluster's list of kept addresses and saves the cluster's balancer without it. The addresses then stay allocated, billed and in the floating IP quota, and no page lists them, also after the roll forward. They go back to the pool only with the cluster. Release each one on the cluster's page before the rollback.

8. **Follow each row of this table that matches your site.**

|---|---|

| A platform on the BOM `0.10.1` or `0.10.1-rc.2` | Four changes, allowed: those two and `cert-manager` `v1.20.2` → `v1.21.3` on the seed and on the platform cluster. Back up the cert-manager resources first, as the [notes of v0.10.2](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.10.2) describe. Read, not run. |
| A platform on the BOM `0.10.1-rc.1` | Five changes, allowed: those four and the platform cluster's chart `0.1.15` → `0.1.16`. Read, not run. |

| A platform on the BOM `0.11.1-rc.1` | The plan lists one change, the release, record only (step 3). Read, not run. |

## What changed

Since `v0.11.1-rc.1`: three merged pull requests that change the product (#446, #447, #448). They are described under "Fixed since rc.1". A fourth changes only the repository's own tests, which now pull each published chart once per machine (#449). The rest of this section is what `v0.11.1` holds since `v0.11.0`, as the notes of rc.1 listed it: fifteen merged pull requests, four of them sandbox work for `v0.12.0` that is switched off here (see "Sandboxes").

### Load balancers

- **A protocol per listener and the layer 4 options** (#431). Per listener: `protocol`, `connectionLimit` (1 to 20000) and `timeoutClientData`. Per pool: `sessionPersistence` by source address, `proxyVersion` 1 or 2, `timeoutMemberConnect` and `timeoutMemberData`. Per health check: the types `tcp`, `http`, `https`, `tls-hello` and `none`, and `interval`, `timeout`, `rise` and `fall`. A field at its default writes no line. See [the load balancer section of the cloud integration guide](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.2/docs/cloud-integration.md#load-balancers-haproxy-appliance).
- **UDP listeners on a pair** (#433). IPVS in NAT mode balances the datagrams. A second keepalived, in a unit of its own, fills its table and runs only health checks. The keepalived that moves the address is not touched: a server that comes or goes reloads the UDP service alone. Round robin, least connections or source address, with weights; persistence by source address; source ranges.
- **UDP health checks** (#433). `udp-connect` is the default: a datagram to the port and a ping. It takes an optional `payload` and `requireReply`. `tcp` or `http` on a port of your choice, or `none`. A server leaves after 2 to 10 failed checks.
- **The appliance is no router** (#433). Forwarding is on only while an nftables chain with policy drop is loaded that lets through nothing but the answers of balanced flows.
- **TLS and HTTPS listeners, and a project's certificates** (#434). Upload a PEM chain and its key, read what a certificate says, delete one no listener names. RSA of 2048 to 8192 bits or ECDSA P-256 or P-384, no passphrase, a chain of five at most. There is no update: a replacement is a new certificate named on the listeners in the old one's place. A change of certificate is one reload and closes no connection.
- **`https`** offers HTTP/2 and HTTP/1.1, speaks HTTP/1.1 to the servers and sets `X-Forwarded-For` and `X-Forwarded-Proto: https`. TLS to the servers is not offered.
- **The files of an appliance are compared at every pass** (#433). An appliance whose `haproxy.cfg`, `keepalived.conf` or UDP file is not the one the platform recorded is pushed again. Before, a file changed by hand stayed.
- **No request is lost at a reload or when a node changes** (#430). A reload keeps the state of every member, backends are named by pool, the controller sends a node from its first Ready on, and a refused connect is tried on another member at once.
- **A Service's address is written when it is served** (#430). The controller wrote it as soon as it was allocated, minutes before the first answer.
- **Smaller fixes of the second drill of `v0.11.0`** (#430). A balancer's lock is a lease of one minute. A paused VM is a known state. A pair with a paused appliance names the pause. An appliance whose HAProxy has no listeners gives up the address. Three messages are corrected.
- **The console** has the protocol list, the options, the UDP checks with their caveat, and a Certificates page.

### Managed Kubernetes

- **Gateway protocol 2** (#435). It only adds to protocol 1: a Service's annotations, affinity and chosen nodes in the request, its `ipMode` and Events in the answer. A controller of `v0.11.0` is served as before.
- **UDP and mixed Services** (#435). A Service's UDP ports are served by the pair's LVS. A TCP and a UDP port of one number share the Service's address. An SCTP port refuses the Service with `ProtocolNotSupported`.
- **Session affinity** (#435). `sessionAffinity: ClientIP` keeps a client on one node for the Service's timeout, for TCP and UDP.
- **TLS from a Service's Secret** (#438). `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` names one to four `kubernetes.io/tls` Secrets of the Service's namespace. The controller reads only the Secrets its Services name. A renewed Secret reaches both appliances as a new file and one reload. A Secret that is deleted or bad never takes TLS off a port and never costs a Service its address. See ["TLS from a Service's Secret"](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.2/docs/managed-k8s.md#tls-from-a-services-secret).
- **Keeping an address** (#440). `proxcloud.io/lb-keep-address: "true"` leaves the Service's address to its cluster when the Service goes. A Service that names a kept address in `spec.loadBalancerIP` gets it. A kept address is billed and counts in the floating IP quota until it is released. A cluster keeps 5 at most.
- **The controller watches** (#439). It watches the Services, the Nodes and the TLS Secrets its Services name, and takes its turn one second after a change. The watch is only a trigger: a turn still lists everything and sends the whole state. An unchanged state is sent every 30 s, where it was every 10 s.
- **Which nodes are behind a Service follows Kubernetes' own rule** (#435). The exclude label is read as a boolean, and a node with the cluster autoscaler's removal taint is taken out.
- **Four review findings closed** (#441). `proxcloud.io/lb-internal` with a value that is no boolean refuses the Service. An empty `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` asks for TLS and is held like a bad Secret. The second of two quick changes no longer waits about 11 s. The console holds the API's rule for "Down after" on a UDP pool.
- **Chart `0.1.19` for new clusters, and the pages** (#442). Both consoles show each port's protocol, the Secret and expiry of a certificate, the kept addresses, and which controller a cluster has.

The annotations, all under `proxcloud.io/lb-`. No other name is read.

| `proxcloud.io/lb-` | Values | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `proxy-protocol` | `true` or `v2`, `v1`, `false` | PROXY header to the nodes on the TCP ports. `true` is version 2, as in `v0.11.0` |
| `method` | `roundrobin`, `leastconn`, `source` | The algorithm |
| `connection-limit` | 1 to 20000, `-1` = none | Open connections per listener; per node on a UDP port |
| `timeout-client-data`, `timeout-member-connect`, `timeout-member-data` | 1000 to 86400000 ms | The three timeouts of the TCP ports |
| `x-forwarded-for` | `true` | The TCP ports become HTTP listeners that add `X-Forwarded-For` |
| `enable-health-monitor` | `true`, `false` | `false` = no health check; not obeyed for policy Local |
| `health-monitor-delay`, `-timeout`, `-max-retries`, `-max-retries-down` | 1 to 300 s, 1 to 300 s, 1 to 10, 1 to 10 | The check's interval, timeout, good checks to come up, bad checks to go down |
| `node-selector` | `key=value, key` | Only the nodes with every one of these labels are behind the Service |
| `keep-address` | `true`, `false` | The address stays the cluster's when the Service goes |
| `internal` | `true`, `false` | `true` refuses the Service: it never gets a public address |
| `tls-secret` | one to four Secret names, between commas | TLS ends on the balancer with these certificates; the first answers a client that asks for no name |
| `tls-ports` | TCP ports, between commas | Only these ports end TLS; unset, every TCP port does |
| `backend-protocol` | `http`, `tcp` | What the nodes are spoken to in behind a port that ends TLS |

- **An unknown name of the prefix** is named in one Warning Event `AnnotationNotSupported`. A value that is not understood is named in `InvalidAnnotation` and the default holds. Neither refuses a Service. `internal` is the one annotation that takes an address back.
- The full table, the differences from OCCM and how to rename a manifest are in [the managed Kubernetes guide](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.2/docs/managed-k8s.md#9-how-a-cluster-lands-on-proxmox).

## Verified on a lab

Nothing ran on this candidate: its images do not exist before the tag. Everything below ran on one lab **on `v0.11.1-rc.1`**, on 2026-11-23, in the two parts named. This candidate holds that code with the fixes above on top, and none of the fixes has run on a lab.

- **Where.** A lab of three Proxmox nodes with a public test range. Appliances on Ubuntu 26.04 with HAProxy 3.2.25. A cluster made by `v0.11.0` with its appliance pair, and one single balancer made by `v0.11.0`.

### On rc.1: the upgrade from v0.11.0

- **The portal upgrade under five client loops.** `proxcloud-up.sh upgrade` ran 30 s and took its backup first. Five loops sent a request every 0.1 s, to four Services of the cluster and to the single balancer. Across the portal upgrade and the pushes each loop counted 11454 to 11457 requests and 0 failed. The loop on the Service that stayed served to the end of the part counted **31365 requests, 0 failed**, across the portal upgrade, three pushes, the platform run, the cluster's update and eight changes of the listener list.
- **One push per balancer.** The pair's standby was pushed first, and the appliance that holds the address 15 s after it. Each got keepalived's files with one keepalived reload, and the HAProxy config with one HAProxy reload. There was no VRRP transition, no restart and no new package. The single balancer was left alone for ten minutes and was pushed once, by the first services sync. Nothing was pushed a second time in the 16 minutes watched after it.
- **The config change** was the four things step 2 names. The one new file on an appliance was the state file.
- **The platform run of two record-only rows.** The plan from `0.11.0` listed the release and the tenant chart, nothing else. The run succeeded in 44 s. All 18 running VMs kept their QEMU process.
- **K12: a cluster on the `v0.11.0` controller changes in nothing.** After the portal upgrade and the platform run its four TCP Services kept their addresses and answered. No annotation was read. Its UDP Service stayed refused with `UDPNotSupported`, with no address and no bill row. Both consoles' API named the controller's protocol, 1 of 2.
- **The cluster's update without a machine roll.** The tenant's platform update moved the cluster to chart `0.1.19` and the new controller within half a minute. The VMs' QEMU processes and SMBIOS UUIDs, the node's UID and boot ID were unchanged, and the tenant's 11 pods were not touched.
- **The `lb-internal` cases.** At the update a Service with `"true"` lost its address with `InternalNotSupported`, and one with `"yes"` lost it with `InvalidAnnotation`. Neither lost it at the portal upgrade.
- **The keep-address case.** A Service with `proxcloud.io/lb-keep-address` was deleted. Its address left both appliances and stayed the cluster's, billed and counted. A new Service that named it in `spec.loadBalancerIP` got it back with the project at 5 of 5 addresses, with no quota refusal.
- **The `loadBalancerIP` cases.** A Service that named a free address the cluster does not keep, and one that named the single balancer's address, each got an address of the pool and a `LoadBalancerIPIgnored` Warning.
- **A UDP Service answering from outside.** After the update the refused UDP Service got its address without anyone acting. From a VM outside the cluster a DNS query was answered `NOERROR`, and 50 of 50 datagrams were answered. The UDP service, its nftables table and forwarding came up on both appliances, with no package installed and no HAProxy reload.
- **What did not hold on rc.1.** That Service's status never showed its address, and the pair read `DEGRADED`: the defect fixed above.

### On rc.1: TLS on a single balancer

- **Uploads.** RSA 2048, ECDSA P-256, a chain of three and an expired certificate (with its warning) were accepted. A mismatched key, a bundle over the limit and a chain in the wrong order were refused. No answer of the API held key material.
- **SNI.** An `https` listener with two certificates gave each name its own. No name and an unknown name got the first.
- **Versions.** TLS 1.0 and 1.1 were refused, 1.2 and 1.3 accepted. CBC, RSA key exchange and DHE suites were refused.
- **HTTP/2** was negotiated. The server received `X-Forwarded-Proto: https` and the client's address in `X-Forwarded-For`.
- **PROXY v1 and v2.** Behind two `tls` listeners the server read the client's address and port from the header.
- **Rotation under load.** 50 requests a second and a 300 MB download ran while the certificate was replaced and the old one deleted: **0 failed of 6450**, and the download completed. The new certificate was served 7.9 s after the call.
- **The 16 KiB bundle.** A chain of five RSA 4096 certificates with its key, 16371 bytes, reached the appliance byte for byte, on a listener with ten certificates. A client verified it against the test root.
- **The search for the private key.** 289 strings of the test keys, in three encodings, were searched in the database dump, the sealed column, the saved API answers, the container logs, the audit trail, the logs and journals of the three Proxmox nodes, the VM's config and cloud-init, and the whole filesystem of the appliance (120668 files). **0 hits outside the sealed row and the certificate files**: the database holds a key only sealed, and on the appliance it was in exactly the 11 certificate files, each mode 600 of root in a directory of mode 700. Not searched: free blocks of the appliance's disk, process memory, and the storage under the VM disk.
- **A reboot of the appliance.** HAProxy came back by itself with all eleven certificates and no push. The balancer did not answer for 56 s.
- **What the part found.** A certificate file damaged by hand went unnoticed: the fix above. Not shown there: a config refused with a TLS listener, and a damaged file at HAProxy's start.

## Known issues

### UDP

- **UDP only on a pair.** A balancer on one VM answers `400` "UDP needs a high-availability load balancer".
- **A UDP server never sees the client's address.** It sees the appliance's private address. UDP has no PROXY protocol.
- **The default UDP check has blind spots.** Without a `payload` it sends random bytes to the service. Without `requireReply`, a closed port on a running server is not seen while the port has traffic. A server whose firewall drops silently is never seen as down without a reply check.
- **The default UDP check needs ICMP.** It pings the server. A server that does not answer a ping from the appliance is taken out, even when its UDP port works.
- **After a failover the other appliance spreads UDP clients afresh.** The appliances do not sync their connections. Source persistence starts afresh too, for TCP as well, and after every change of the configuration.
- **The connection limit of a UDP listener counts per server**, not per listener.

### TLS

- **Where private keys rest.** In the database, encrypted with the portal's encryption key. In `backups/`, where the database dump and a copy of `.env` with `ENCRYPTION_KEY` lie side by side: whoever reads that directory reads every tenant's private key. And in clear on each appliance's disk (root only), so in any Proxmox backup, snapshot, clone or replica of an appliance VM.
- **A Service's Secret rests the same way.** The portal seals it in a row of the cluster's balancer and writes it to both appliances.
- **Whoever may write a Service in a namespace can have any TLS Secret of that namespace served**, as with an Ingress.
- **No update of a certificate.** Rotation is: upload the new one, name it on the listeners, delete the old one.
- **The TLS policy is fixed.** No TLS 1.1, no RSA key exchange, no CBC suite. No TLS to the servers.
- **A certificate with a signature the appliance's HAProxy refuses** is taken by the upload and refused at the push. The last config stays in use and the row says why.
- **While a Secret is unusable its whole Service is frozen** as it was served, its node list included when it has a node selector.

### Kubernetes

- **A request for an internal balancer under another cloud's annotation name is not recognised.** Only `proxcloud.io/lb-internal` is read. A Service that asks for an internal balancer with OCCM's annotation alone gets a **public** address. Rename the annotation before you apply such a manifest.
- **Annotations of any other prefix do nothing**, are not sent to the portal and raise no Event. `proxcloud.io/lb-proxy-protocol: "true"` is version 2 here; write `v1` for version 1.
- **Chart `0.1.19` with a `v0.11.0` controller image does not run.** The chart starts the controller with `--watch=true`, and that image exits at it. The one way into this pair is a portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` that is then asked to enable the controller again, or to set the chart version, on a cluster that is on `0.1.19`. Do neither while the portal is rolled back. The Services of such a cluster stay as they were last served.
- **A portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` takes the address of a TLS Service** and gives it a new one after the roll forward. Each turn of a new controller is then two requests, and the old portal audits the refused one.
- **A portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` drops the list of kept addresses.** They stay allocated, billed and in the floating IP quota, and no page lists them, also after the roll forward, until the cluster is deleted. Release them before a rollback (step 7).
- **An existing cluster is not upgraded for you.** It keeps protocol 1 until its chart is moved (step 5). **Enable load balancer controller**, which also rotates the credential, moves it too: its refused UDP Services then get addresses without the tenant acting.
- **A cluster on the `v0.11.0` controller that adds `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` serves the port plain**, with no Event: that controller sends no such annotation.
- **A cluster's state over 64 KiB is refused as a whole.** Nothing changes and every address stays; each Service gets a Warning Event `TooLarge`.
- **The gateway has no rate limit per cluster.** A Service that flaps causes about one call a second, where it was one per 10 s.
- **"The controller last reported" reads up to a minute and a half.** A degraded pair of a quiet cluster is looked at in steps of 30 s, where it was 10 s.
- **Pool names of a cluster's balancer changed.** Nothing reads them but people.

### Open from the drill of rc.1 and the reviews of its fixes

- **A lone appliance of a pair sends one VRRP advert a second to its network's gateway.** It is addressed to `127.0.0.1` and reaches no other host. A gateway that logs such packets may log one a second while a pair has one appliance.
- **A second appliance that joins can hold the addresses beside the first for one pass**, when the push that tells the first of its peer fails in the pass where the new one starts.
- **A change made while an appliance joins or is replaced reaches the appliance that holds the address first**, not the standby.
- **A lone appliance whose public network was taken from the project stays `BUILD`**, and then reads the general "VMs did not become ready".
- **Why a cluster's second appliance was not built is in the API log only.** A tenant's own pair is still taken back as a whole when its second appliance cannot be built.
- **A reload that keeps HAProxy's master socket closed for more than 0.6 s is still one failed run of keepalived's track script.** Two in a row move the addresses.
- **A support file of keepalived that is edited by hand is not seen by a pass.** `keepalived.conf`, `haproxy.cfg`, the UDP files and the certificate files are.
- **While an appliance holds a change it refused, its certificate files are not checked.** The next push it takes writes them.
- **A single balancer whose heal cannot be pushed reads `DOWN`** with the file's message, although HAProxy still serves from memory.
- **Only the last `X-Forwarded-For` value is the balancer's.** A client's own value stays in front of it. Use the PROXY protocol for an address a client cannot forge.
- **A Service can be refused `QuotaExceeded` for a minute or two after other Services gave their addresses back.** It gets its address by itself. Seen on the lab under rc.1.
- **A tenant's cluster row names the old chart version until the next provider sync** after the cluster's update. Seen on the lab under rc.1.

### Still true from v0.11.0

- **A member new to a config starts as up.** A node that is Ready and does not forward yet gets connections until its first check fails. Each waits the connect timeout, 2 s, and is then answered by another member.
- **One pair per cluster**, shared by all its Services. An in-cluster client of a Service with `ipMode: VIP` never passes the pair. The built-in quota is five public addresses.
- **The other known issues** in the [notes of v0.11.0](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.0) still hold, except "UDP is refused", "no TLS termination" and the lost requests at a reload, which this candidate answers by its code.

### Riding along

- **The sandbox invoice line.** A bill with sandbox items that round to 0.00 gets one summed item from now on (see "Billing"). No sent bill changes.
- **Sandbox platform code, switched off.** It cannot be turned on in this candidate: no BOM names its chart.
