ProxCloud v0.11.1-rc.4 is the fourth release candidate of `v0.11.1`, published as a prerelease. `v0.11.1` completes layer 4 of the load balancer: UDP, TLS termination with certificates, and the layer 4 options, for a tenant's own balancers and for the Services of a managed Kubernetes cluster. The lab drill of [v0.11.1-rc.3](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.1-rc.3) ran every part of its plan: the upgrade from rc.2 did what its notes said, the three fixes of rc.3 held, and no regression against `v0.11.0` or rc.2 was found. It found one defect that should not reach the release: a UDP client that already sends when its listener is added got no answer. This candidate holds the fix for it and one small fix of the health view. Neither has run on a lab yet. It is not for production. `v0.11.0` stays the current stable release and `latest`.

The BOM (bill of materials) is the list of exact versions a managed-services platform runs. The BOM of this candidate is `0.11.1-rc.4`. Its pins are those of `0.11.1-rc.1`, `0.11.1-rc.2` and `0.11.1-rc.3`. Against `0.11.0` it moves one thing: the cluster chart for new tenant clusters, `proxmox-kamaji-cluster` `0.1.17` → `0.1.19`.

## Highlights

- **Billing: from `v0.11.0`, rc.1 or rc.2, read the first part of "Upgrade" before you upgrade.** A public address of a Kubernetes Service, or one a cluster keeps, that has no floating IP row gets one at the first pass after the upgrade. It is billed from the time the address was allocated, not from the upgrade. A site that runs rc.3 has these rows already.
- **The drill finding of rc.3 is fixed.** A UDP client that already sends to an address and port when the listener for that port is added is answered. With rc.3 it got no answer for as long as it kept sending. See "Fixed since rc.3".
- **An appliance the platform started reads up once it serves again.** After a failover the first health read takes the mark back. With rc.3 it stayed until the next pass. See "Fixed since rc.3".
- **From rc.3, the upgrade pushes one thing.** A pair with a UDP listener gets one file, the nftables table of its UDP service, with one reload of that service per appliance, the standby first. Every other balancer gets nothing.
- **A UDP port without a listener is silent now.** On a pair with a UDP listener, a datagram for a port that has no UDP listener gets no ICMP "port unreachable" any more. See "From `v0.11.1-rc.3`" in step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **The three drill findings of rc.2 are fixed, and their fixes held on the lab under rc.3.** A change no longer waits while the image's package manager runs on an appliance. A changed connection limit of a UDP listener reaches the kernel. An address annotated to be kept and deleted within a second is kept. The [notes of v0.11.1-rc.3](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.1-rc.3) describe them.
- **From rc.2, a pair with a UDP listener is pushed once.** It gets its UDP config, one new drop-in and the table, with one reload of the UDP service per appliance, the standby first. A limit that was stale is put right by that push. Every other balancer gets nothing.
- **Two of the fixes of rc.3 are in the cluster's controller.** A cluster keeps the controller it has. One that is on chart `0.1.19` under rc.1 or rc.2 is not offered an update by the tenant's console: see step 5 of "Upgrade". Nothing the controller does changed since rc.3.
- **Five listener protocols.** A listener is `tcp`, `udp`, `tls`, `http` or `https`. `v0.11.0` served `tcp` and `http` and refused the rest.
- **UDP, on a pair.** A highly available load balancer takes UDP listeners. The kernel's IPVS (LVS) balances the datagrams on the appliance that holds the address, next to HAProxy. A TCP and a UDP listener may share a port number. A balancer on one VM refuses UDP.
- **TLS ends on the balancer.** A project uploads certificates with their keys. A `tls` listener passes TCP to the servers, an `https` listener passes HTTP. One listener presents up to ten certificates and picks by the server name the client asks for (SNI). TLS 1.2 and 1.3 only, with a fixed list of suites.
- **The layer 4 options.** Session persistence by source address, PROXY protocol version 1 or 2, health checks of type `tcp`, `http`, `https`, `tls-hello` and `none` with their own interval, timeout, rise and fall, three timeouts, and a connection limit per listener.
- **Kubernetes Services get the same.** A Service of a managed cluster can have UDP ports, TCP and UDP on one address, session affinity, TLS from a `kubernetes.io/tls` Secret, and a set of `proxcloud.io/lb-*` annotations. A Service can also leave its public address to its cluster and take it again later.
- **The controller watches.** With cluster chart `0.1.19` a change of a Service or a node is sent to the portal about a second after it happens. Before, the controller listed the cluster every 10 s.
- **An existing cluster gets none of this by the portal's upgrade.** It keeps the controller it was made with until it is moved to chart `0.1.19`. Until then its UDP Services stay refused, so nothing gets an address and a bill without its owner's action. See step 5 of "Upgrade".
- **From `v0.11.0`, every load balancer is reloaded once after the upgrade.** The HAProxy config changed for every balancer, so each one is pushed once, the standby first. See step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **From rc.1, keepalived on a pair is reloaded once more.** The HAProxy config is the same to the byte. See step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **No request is lost at a reload or a node change any more** (#430). `v0.11.0` listed this as a known issue. On the lab a client loop counted 31365 requests and 0 failed across the upgrade to rc.1, 24627 and 0 failed across the upgrade from rc.1 to rc.2, and eight loops counted 183773 requests and 0 failed in the 47 minutes around the upgrade from rc.2 to rc.3. A node change under load was not run there.
- **Only `proxcloud.io/lb-*` annotations are read.** The product defines its own names. A manifest written for another cloud's controller must be renamed first: see Known issues.
- **What ran on a lab ran on rc.1, on rc.2 and on rc.3.** "Verified on a lab" names the candidate of each part. Nothing of this candidate's own two fixes has run on a lab.

## Fixed since rc.3

One pull request (#473), from the lab drill of rc.3. The finding of the drill first:

- **A UDP client that was sending before its listener existed got no answer.** A UDP client that already sent to an address and port from one source port when the listener for that port was added got no answer for as long as it kept sending. The balancer read `ACTIVE`, the members `UP`, and a new client was answered, so nothing showed it. On the lab, under rc.3, none of 2584 datagrams was answered in the 8 minutes after the listener was live. rc.1 and rc.2 have the same table. `v0.11.0` has no UDP.
- **Whom it caught.** Every client that sends from one source port and is never silent for 30 s, such as a syslog, flow or metrics exporter or a VPN peer. A client that was caught was answered again only once it was silent for more than 30 s, or sent from another port. A client whose listener existed first was not affected.
- **Three ways in.** A UDP listener, or a Service's UDP port, added while clients send. A restart of `nftables.service` on the appliance that holds the address: 4933 datagrams went unanswered on the lab. And a UDP Service made again on a kept address, which is on the appliance some seconds before its listener: this one was not tried on the lab.
- **Why.** The appliance remembered the client's flow from the first datagram that found no listener, without the address rewrite the UDP path needs. Every later datagram kept that entry alive. Once the listener was there, the server got the client's own address as the source and sent its answer by the tenant's gateway, where it was lost.
- **Now.** The nftables table of the UDP service has one more chain, `lb-input`. It drops a first UDP datagram from outside that the balancer did not take, before the flow is remembered. The first datagram after the listener is served is answered. The kernel's own table decides, so nothing has to follow the listeners, and no package is added.
- **Measured in real guests, not on a lab.** The userland of Debian 13 and of Ubuntu 26.04 on the test host's kernel, with a client that sends five datagrams a second from one socket. Without the chain: 0 answers in 62 s. With it: the first answer 0.6 to 0.8 s after the push that added the listener, 0.0 to 0.2 s after the members were back in the table at a restart of `nftables.service`, and 0.7 to 0.8 s after the push for an address that came 7 s before its listener. A client of a listener that was there first lost nothing meanwhile.
- **A table without the chain is noticed.** An appliance whose loaded table lacks the chain's drop, from a release before or changed by hand, reads up with a change to load, and its next pass loads the file again.
- **What it changes on the wire.** A UDP datagram for a port without a listener is dropped without an answer. See "From `v0.11.1-rc.3`" in step 2 of "Upgrade" and Known issues.

A smaller fix of the same drill, in the same pull request:

- **An appliance the platform started reads up once it serves again.** After a failover, the appliance whose VM was stopped and which the platform started read `down` "its VM is stopped; starting it", and the balancer `DEGRADED`, until the next pass after it was back. On a tenant's own pair that is the next services sync. On a cluster's pair it is the controller's next turn: `ACTIVE` 1 min 15 s after the shutdown on the lab. Now the first health read that reaches the appliance and finds it serving takes the mark back, in its answer and in the balancer's row: the balancer's page, or a `GET_HEALTH` call. The mark "it does not answer" is taken back the same way.
- **What "serving" means there.** HAProxy serves its configuration, keepalived is master or backup, and on a pair with a UDP listener the UDP service serves. That last condition is new for the health read's own mark "it cannot be reached" too.
- **One case waits for a pass.** An appliance that was down while a change was loaded on the other one keeps reading down until a pass has loaded the change on it too. Otherwise it would read up while it serves the configuration of before the change.

## Upgrade

Use a lab or another non-production site. Production sites should stay on `v0.11.0` and wait for `v0.11.1`. These steps upgrade from `v0.11.0`, from `v0.11.1-rc.1`, from `v0.11.1-rc.2` or from `v0.11.1-rc.3`. From an older release, read the [notes of v0.11.0](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.0) first: their steps and cautions still apply. The figures below were measured on one lab under rc.1, rc.2 or rc.3, as named. Nothing was measured on this candidate.

**Billing first. This holds from `v0.11.0`, from rc.1 and from rc.2. A site that runs rc.3 has it behind it: its passes wrote these rows.**

- **Addresses that have no floating IP row get one, billed from their allocation time.** The floating IP row (`FLOATING_IP`) of a public address is what lists the address for the tenant and what bills it. Until rc.3 only the services sync (the changelog calls it the provider sync) wrote the row of an address that a cluster's balancer holds for a Service, or that a cluster keeps. Since rc.3 the balancer's own pass writes it.
- **When.** At the first pass of each cluster's balancer after the portal's upgrade: its controller's next request, within 30 s, or the services sync. Nobody has to act, and no plan row announces it.
- **From when it bills.** The row carries the time the address was allocated, not the time of the upgrade. Its bill item is counted from that time, or from the start of the open bill's cycle where the address is older than that. With a price by the hour, an address allocated ten days ago in this cycle is billed for those ten days at the next charge run.
- **It is the row a services sync writes.** A sync before the upgrade writes the same rows with the same start. The upgrade bills nothing a sync would not have billed. It bills it at the first pass, and without a sync.
- **Which sites this changes.** On a site whose services sync runs on schedule, at most the addresses taken since its last run. On a site that runs no sync by itself, such as a lab with the scheduler off, every address of a Service and every kept address that no sync has seen: they counted in the quota and were neither listed nor billed. On the lab under rc.2, 12 addresses were in use with 4 rows until a sync ran.
- **What it does not touch.** A tenant's own balancers and the public addresses of servers: their rows are the sync's, as before. No address moves, and nothing is pushed to an appliance for this.
- **What to check before the upgrade, on rc.2.** For each project with a cluster, read `GET /api/v1/project/{id}/quota-usage`. `network.addresses` lists every public address the quota counts, with `since` and `kind`. Compare the rows of kind `service` and `kept` with the project's floating IPs. Each one that has no floating IP is billed from its `since` after the upgrade.
- **What to check before the upgrade, on `v0.11.0` or rc.1.** They have no such list. Run a services sync and read the project's floating IPs and its open bill: the sync writes the rows the upgrade would write.
- **There is no switch for it.** An address that is given back before the upgrade gets no row: its Service deleted, or the kept address released on the cluster's page. An address that stays is billed from its allocation, by this pass or by the next sync.
- **On the lab, at the upgrade from rc.2 to rc.3,** six addresses got their floating IP row with the allocation time as its start. What a charge run then bills was read in the code only: the rows were read, and no charge run was made.

   **From `v0.11.0`: every load balancer is pushed once.**

   - **Why.** The config the platform renders for an existing balancer is no longer the one `v0.11.0` rendered: it names a state file, tries a refused connect on another member, waits 2 s for a connect where it waited 5 s, and names its backends by pool. The files of a pair's keepalived changed too. So each balancer's recorded hash differs, and its next pass pushes it.
   - **What a push is.** One HAProxy reload per appliance, and one keepalived reload per appliance of a pair. A reload is not a restart: open connections are kept.
   - **The standby first.** A pair's standby gets the config before the appliance that holds the address. A config that HAProxy on the standby does not take is not sent to the other one. On each appliance the apply checks the file, reloads, proves that HAProxy listens on every address of it, and puts the old file back otherwise.
   - **What it costs once.** This reload is the last one that starts every member as up. A balancer with a dead member, or a Service with `externalTrafficPolicy: Local`, sees the known 2 to 4 seconds of lost requests once more.
   - **Until its push**, the health view of a balancer lists its members as `NOT LOADED`. The listeners are not affected.
   - **A balancer without a UDP or TLS listener gets no new package and one new file**, the state file.
   - **sshd is masked by that push**, on every appliance.
   - **No balancer of `v0.11.0` has a UDP listener**, so nothing of "From `v0.11.1-rc.2`" and "From `v0.11.1-rc.3`" below applies to it.
   - **This ran on the lab under rc.1**, as described: see "Verified on a lab". This candidate renders the same HAProxy config and the same keepalived files as rc.2 and rc.3.

   **From `v0.11.1-rc.1`: a pair gets keepalived's files once more, and what follows for rc.2 and rc.3.**

   - **Why.** Two files of a pair changed between rc.1 and rc.2: keepalived's track script and its notify script. `keepalived.conf` is the same to the byte.
   - **What happens.** The first pass writes keepalived's five files to each appliance of a pair and reloads keepalived once per appliance, the standby first. A reload keeps keepalived's state.
   - **HAProxy is not touched.** `haproxy.cfg` is the same to the byte as under rc.1, so it is not pushed and HAProxy is not reloaded.
   - **A single balancer gets nothing.** It runs no keepalived.
   - **A pair degraded by the rc.1 defect becomes `ACTIVE` at that first pass.** Its cluster's pending Services become ready.
   - **A pair with a UDP listener also gets the push described for rc.2**, in the same pass, with the table of rc.3's part in it.
   - **This ran on the lab, from rc.1 to rc.2**: see "Verified on a lab". From rc.1 to this candidate it was read from the code and is held by tests.

   **From `v0.11.1-rc.2`: a pair with a UDP listener gets its UDP config, a drop-in and the table once. Nothing else is pushed.**

Its HAProxy config, `keepalived.conf` and keepalived's support files are the same to the byte as under rc.2.
   - **A pair with a UDP listener is pushed once**, a tenant's or a cluster's, at its first pass after the upgrade, the standby first and then the appliance that holds the address.
   - **What is written.** `/etc/keepalived/lvs.conf`, which gains one line, `warmup 0`, in every virtual server that has a health check. A new drop-in, `/etc/systemd/system/proxcloud-lvs.service.d/nftables.conf`, which ties the UDP service to `nftables.service`. And `/etc/proxcloud-lb/lvs.nft`, the table, which gains the chain `lb-input`: see "From `v0.11.1-rc.3`" below for what it does. The other three files of the UDP service are the same and are not written. A pair whose UDP pools all have the check switched off gets the drop-in and the table.
   - **What is reloaded.** systemd's unit files, for the drop-in, and the UDP service `proxcloud-lvs.service`, once per appliance. The unit is not restarted. HAProxy and the keepalived that moves the address are not touched, so no address moves.
   - **What the reload does to traffic.** The service loads its nftables table again in one transaction, and keepalived's check process reads the config again. Members that are up stay in the kernel's table through it. On the lab, from rc.2 to rc.3, no client loop lost a request across this push, on four appliances. The table's file was the same then.    - **When the standby cannot take the push**, the balancer stays `ACTIVE` and says "A change waits", the appliance that holds the address is not pushed before the standby, and the platform tries again by itself.

   - **The package-manager fix pushes no file.** It changed the commands the portal sends, not a file on an appliance. An appliance meets them at its next push.
   - **The balancer's row gets two fields**, written without a push: `heldSince` while a change waits, and `served` on each Service of a cluster's balancer.

   - **A pair with a UDP listener is pushed once**, a tenant's or a cluster's, at its first pass after the upgrade, the standby first and then the appliance that holds the address. Until that pass an appliance serves as it did before the upgrade.

4. **Run the platform upgrade.** Type `upgrade <installation name> to 0.11.1-rc.4`. There is one action, with no parts, and no seed backup is asked for.

   - **What it does.** It records the new release. From `0.11.0` it also writes chart `0.1.19` on the Kubernetes provider: clusters created after it get the new chart and the controller that watches.
   - **What it does not do.** It replaces no machine, syncs nothing and writes nothing on Proxmox. The platform cluster stays on chart `0.1.16`. No tenant cluster changes chart, and no balancer is touched by this run.
   - **No sandbox.** The BOM holds no sandbox component, so the run installs none.

   - **A cluster made before `v0.11.1`** keeps the controller of `v0.11.0`. Its UDP Services stay `UDPNotSupported`, and no annotation but `proxcloud.io/lb-proxy-protocol: "true"` is read. ### Load balancers

- **A protocol per listener and the layer 4 options** (#431). Per listener: `protocol`, `connectionLimit` (1 to 20000) and `timeoutClientData`. Per pool: `sessionPersistence` by source address, `proxyVersion` 1 or 2, `timeoutMemberConnect` and `timeoutMemberData`. Per health check: the types `tcp`, `http`, `https`, `tls-hello` and `none`, and `interval`, `timeout`, `rise` and `fall`. A field at its default writes no line. See [the load balancer section of the cloud integration guide](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.4/docs/cloud-integration.md#load-balancers-haproxy-appliance).
- **UDP listeners on a pair** (#433). IPVS in NAT mode balances the datagrams. A second keepalived, in a unit of its own, fills its table and runs only health checks. The keepalived that moves the address is not touched: a server that comes or goes reloads the UDP service alone. Round robin, least connections or source address, with weights; persistence by source address; source ranges.
- **UDP health checks** (#433). `udp-connect` is the default: a datagram to the port and a ping. It takes an optional `payload` and `requireReply`. `tcp` or `http` on a port of your choice, or `none`. A server leaves after 2 to 10 failed checks.
- **The appliance is no router** (#433). Forwarding is on only while an nftables chain with policy drop is loaded that lets through nothing but the answers of balanced flows. Since this candidate the same table drops a UDP datagram for a port without a listener (#473).
- **TLS and HTTPS listeners, and a project's certificates** (#434). Upload a PEM chain and its key, read what a certificate says, delete one no listener names. RSA of 2048 to 8192 bits or ECDSA P-256 or P-384, no passphrase, a chain of five at most. There is no update: a replacement is a new certificate named on the listeners in the old one's place. A change of certificate is one reload and closes no connection.
- **`https`** offers HTTP/2 and HTTP/1.1, speaks HTTP/1.1 to the servers and sets `X-Forwarded-For` and `X-Forwarded-Proto: https`. TLS to the servers is not offered.
- **The files of an appliance are compared at every pass** (#433). An appliance whose `haproxy.cfg`, `keepalived.conf` or UDP file is not the one the platform recorded is pushed again. Before, a file changed by hand stayed.
- **No request is lost at a reload or when a node changes** (#430). A reload keeps the state of every member, backends are named by pool, the controller sends a node from its first Ready on, and a refused connect is tried on another member at once.
- **A Service's address is written when it is served** (#430). The controller wrote it as soon as it was allocated, minutes before the first answer.
- **Smaller fixes of the second drill of `v0.11.0`** (#430). A balancer's lock is a lease of one minute. A paused VM is a known state. A pair with a paused appliance names the pause. An appliance whose HAProxy has no listeners gives up the address. Three messages are corrected.
- **The console** has the protocol list, the options, the UDP checks with their caveat, and a Certificates page.

### Managed Kubernetes

- **Gateway protocol 2** (#435). It only adds to protocol 1: a Service's annotations, affinity and chosen nodes in the request, its `ipMode` and Events in the answer. A controller of `v0.11.0` is served as before.
- **UDP and mixed Services** (#435). A Service's UDP ports are served by the pair's LVS. A TCP and a UDP port of one number share the Service's address. An SCTP port refuses the Service with `ProtocolNotSupported`.
- **Session affinity** (#435). `sessionAffinity: ClientIP` keeps a client on one node for the Service's timeout, for TCP and UDP.
- **TLS from a Service's Secret** (#438). `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` names one to four `kubernetes.io/tls` Secrets of the Service's namespace. The controller reads only the Secrets its Services name. A renewed Secret reaches both appliances as a new file and one reload. A Secret that is deleted or bad never takes TLS off a port and never costs a Service its address. See ["TLS from a Service's Secret"](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.4/docs/managed-k8s.md#tls-from-a-services-secret).
- **Keeping an address** (#440). `proxcloud.io/lb-keep-address: "true"` leaves the Service's address to its cluster when the Service goes. A Service that names a kept address in `spec.loadBalancerIP` gets it. A kept address is billed and counts in the floating IP quota until it is released. A cluster keeps 5 at most.
- **The controller watches** (#439). It watches the Services, the Nodes and the TLS Secrets its Services name, and takes its turn one second after a change. The watch is only a trigger: a turn still lists everything and sends the whole state. An unchanged state is sent every 30 s, where it was every 10 s.
- **Which nodes are behind a Service follows Kubernetes' own rule** (#435). The exclude label is read as a boolean, and a node with the cluster autoscaler's removal taint is taken out.
- **Four review findings closed** (#441). `proxcloud.io/lb-internal` with a value that is no boolean refuses the Service. An empty `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` asks for TLS and is held like a bad Secret. The second of two quick changes no longer waits about 11 s. The console holds the API's rule for "Down after" on a UDP pool.
- **Chart `0.1.19` for new clusters, and the pages** (#442). Both consoles show each port's protocol, the Secret and expiry of a certificate, the kept addresses, and which controller a cluster has.

The annotations, all under `proxcloud.io/lb-`. No other name is read.

| `proxcloud.io/lb-` | Values | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `proxy-protocol` | `true` or `v2`, `v1`, `false` | PROXY header to the nodes on the TCP ports. `true` is version 2, as in `v0.11.0` |
| `method` | `roundrobin`, `leastconn`, `source` | The algorithm |
| `connection-limit` | 1 to 20000, `-1` = none | Open connections per listener; per node on a UDP port |

- **The sandbox invoice line** on a sent bill of a real site, and anything of the sandbox platform code.
- **Earlier releases.** Everything the [notes of v0.11.0](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.0) list as not verified still holds.

## Known issues

### UDP

- **UDP only on a pair.** A balancer on one VM answers `400` "UDP needs a high-availability load balancer".
- **A UDP server never sees the client's address.** It sees the appliance's private address. UDP has no PROXY protocol.
- **The default UDP check has blind spots.** Without a `payload` it sends random bytes to the service. Without `requireReply`, a closed port on a running server is not seen while the port has traffic. A server whose firewall drops silently is never seen as down without a reply check.
- **The default UDP check needs ICMP.** It pings the server. A server that does not answer a ping from the appliance is taken out, even when its UDP port works.
- **After a failover the other appliance spreads UDP clients afresh.** The appliances do not sync their connections. Source persistence starts afresh too, for TCP as well, and after every change of the configuration.
- **The connection limit of a UDP listener counts per server**, not per listener.
- **A UDP port without a listener is silent.** On a pair with a UDP listener, a datagram for a port that has no UDP listener, or whose listener's pool has no server at all, gets no ICMP "port unreachable". A listener whose servers are all down answers it as before.

### TLS

- **Where private keys rest.** In the database, encrypted with the portal's encryption key. In `backups/`, where the database dump and a copy of `.env` with `ENCRYPTION_KEY` lie side by side: whoever reads that directory reads every tenant's private key. And in clear on each appliance's disk (root only), so in any Proxmox backup, snapshot, clone or replica of an appliance VM.
- **A Service's Secret rests the same way.** The portal seals it in a row of the cluster's balancer and writes it to both appliances.
- **Whoever may write a Service in a namespace can have any TLS Secret of that namespace served**, as with an Ingress.
- **No update of a certificate.** Rotation is: upload the new one, name it on the listeners, delete the old one.
- **The TLS policy is fixed.** No TLS 1.1, no RSA key exchange, no CBC suite. No TLS to the servers.
- **A certificate with a signature the appliance's HAProxy refuses** is taken by the upload and refused at the push. The last config stays in use and the row says why.
- **While a Secret is unusable its whole Service is frozen** as it was served, its node list included when it has a node selector.

### Kubernetes

- **A request for an internal balancer under another cloud's annotation name is not recognised.** Only `proxcloud.io/lb-internal` is read. A Service that asks for an internal balancer with OCCM's annotation alone gets a **public** address. Rename the annotation before you apply such a manifest.
- **Annotations of any other prefix do nothing**, are not sent to the portal and raise no Event. `proxcloud.io/lb-proxy-protocol: "true"` is version 2 here; write `v1` for version 1.
- **Chart `0.1.19` with a `v0.11.0` controller image does not run.** The chart starts the controller with `--watch=true`, and that image exits at it. The one way into this pair is a portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` that is then asked to enable the controller again, or to set the chart version, on a cluster that is on `0.1.19`. Do neither while the portal is rolled back. The Services of such a cluster stay as they were last served.
- **A portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` takes the address of a TLS Service** and gives it a new one after the roll forward. Each turn of a new controller is then two requests, and the old portal audits the refused one.
- **A portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` drops the list of kept addresses.** They stay allocated, billed and in the floating IP quota, and no page lists them, also after the roll forward, until the cluster is deleted. Release them before a rollback (step 7).
- **An existing cluster is not upgraded for you.** It keeps protocol 1 until its chart is moved (step 5). **Enable load balancer controller**, which also rotates the credential, moves it too: its refused UDP Services then get addresses without the tenant acting.
- **A cluster on the `v0.11.0` controller that adds `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` serves the port plain**, with no Event: that controller sends no such annotation.
- **A cluster's state over 64 KiB is refused as a whole.** Nothing changes and every address stays; each Service gets a Warning Event `TooLarge`.
- **The gateway has no rate limit per cluster.** A Service that flaps causes about one call a second, where it was one per 10 s.
- **"The controller last reported" reads up to a minute and a half.** A degraded pair of a quiet cluster is looked at in steps of 30 s, where it was 10 s.
- **Pool names of a cluster's balancer changed.** Nothing reads them but people.

### From the drill of rc.2

These were seen on the lab under rc.2 and are changed neither by rc.3 nor by this candidate.

- **One busy UDP port hides a closed one on the same server**, for 86 s on the lab. A security group change that stops ping takes a server that is already in service out only at the next UDP change.
- **`leastconn` on UDP counts the flows of the last five minutes.** At a UDP member's removal under load a datagram in flight can be lost: 1 of 71660 on the lab.
- **A deleted cluster's worker stays in the tenant's lists until the next services sync.**
- **With the scheduler off, `DELETE /project/{id}` answers `200` `SCHEDULED_FOR_DELETION` though nothing will delete it.** `/now` answers `409` and says why.
- **A new cluster's first answer came 8 min 42 s after its Services** (9 min 14 s under `v0.11.0`). The appliance's own first boot is that time.

### Fixed or narrowed by this candidate: what remains

- **A UDP client that is caught at the upgrade stays caught** until it was silent for more than 30 s, or sends from another port. The new chain keeps such a flow from being made and frees none. Until a pair's first pass after the upgrade it serves as rc.3 did: add the UDP listener, or create the Service, before clients send to it.
- **The first datagrams after a listener is served can be lost**, for about 0.6 to 0.9 s, until its first member passes its check: 2 to 4 datagrams of a client that sends five a second.
- **A ruleset of your own that tracks connections while the product's table is away** can still catch a client, for example during a restart of `nftables.service`. With no table at all nothing tracks by itself.
- **A restart of `nftables.service` on an appliance with a UDP listener leaves UDP unanswered on it for a second or two** (1.6 s for new clients on the lab, under rc.3). Under rc.3 a client that sent through the restart from one source port, on the appliance that holds the address, was not answered again until it was silent for more than 30 s. With this candidate it is answered once the members are back in the table: 0.0 to 0.2 s after that in real guests, not run on a lab. A start of a stopped `nftables.service` by hand is seen by the next pass only.
- **A masquerade or TCP `notrack` rule taken out of the loaded table by hand is not noticed**, as under rc.3. The chain `lb-input` and the forward chain's drop are read back.
- **The appliance itself hears no UDP it did not ask for.** Nothing on it listens for any. Its DNS and its time sync are answers to its own questions.
- **After a failover, an appliance that the product had to start reads `down` "its VM is stopped; starting it", and the balancer `DEGRADED`, until the first health read after it is back or the next pass.** On a pair that nobody looks at, that is the next pass: on a tenant's own pair the next services sync, on a cluster's pair the controller's next turn.
- **An appliance that was down through a change stays one change behind until the next services sync or the next change.** Nothing queues a pass after the one that started its VM. Until then it reads `down` "its VM is stopped; starting it", and the balancer `DEGRADED`.

### Fixed or narrowed by rc.3: what remains

- **A change still waits in two cases.** While dpkg is at work on a package the appliance itself runs, which lasts seconds, and during the first install of a new appliance. The balancer answers `200` and says that a change waits.
- **A package upgrade that restarts HAProxy in the moment of a change can make a good config read `ERROR`** "refused" until the next pass. A restart of HAProxy or keepalived by `needrestart` after a library upgrade, during a change, is not handled.
- **The platform's own retries are six, 20 s apart.** With dpkg at work on `haproxy` five times in a row the fourth retry landed the change, 139 s after the call.
- **The balancer says "the platform tries again within half a minute" also when the six retries are over.** On a site without a scheduler nothing tries again until the next change.
- **An appliance behind a held standby still gets a new config first** when its own HAProxy is not serving or one of its certificate files is faulty. And after retries that ended with a change still waiting, the next hold lets the appliance that holds the address go ahead at once.
- **In a pass, an appliance whose state cannot be read twice is still marked down**, and the balancer reads `DOWN` when that happens to both.
- **After a failover the balancer's row names the old roles until the next pass or the next health read.** On a tenant's own pair that nobody looks at, that is the next services sync. Traffic is not affected.
- **On an image without `ipvsadm` a changed UDP connection limit still stays in the file only.** Both appliance images bring it with keepalived.
- **A difference in the kernel's UDP table that cannot be put right fails the push** after about 10 s: the balancer reads `ERROR` while UDP is served. Not expected in practice.
- **An appliance whose HAProxy push is refused or held still takes its UDP change in that pass.**
- **When a replace's wait for UDP members ends at 30 s, the address moves all the same**, and only the API log says so.
- **An existing appliance runs sshd after a reboot until its next push**, on the Ubuntu image. Its firewall drops port 22 and it has no authorized key.
- **A cluster on an earlier controller still gives back an address annotated just before its delete**, and still gets the `GatewayRefused` Warning at a rotation (step 5 of "Upgrade"). On such a cluster wait until the cluster's page shows the annotation before the delete.
- **With more than 50 Services deleted at once**, the address of a Service beyond the first 50 is decided from the stored state in that turn, as under rc.2.
- **A portal rolled back to rc.2 answers `413` to a cluster whose state is within about 6 KiB of the 64 KiB limit.** A deleted Service of that cluster stays `Terminating` until the state shrinks or the portal is upgraded.
- **An annotation changed again while a deleted Service still waits for its finalizer may not start a pass.** This is older than rc.3.
- **A real credential failure is said on the Services two minutes late**: a wrong Secret, or a released cluster. The controller's log has it at once.
- **A services sync that lists the addresses in the moment before a pass takes one can archive the row the pass just wrote.** The next pass or sync writes it again, from the allocation time.
- **With two API replicas, a Service change that arrives during a converge on the other replica waits for the controller's next send.**

### Timings

Measured on the lab under rc.3, or under an earlier candidate where named. Nothing was measured on this candidate. HAProxy's own reload, 4 to 5 s on each appliance on the lab, is unchanged.

- **A change is live in 16 to 33 s.** One TCP port 16 to 20 s, one UDP port 19 to 23 s, a TCP and a UDP port together 26 to 30 s (the TCP part is 5 to 6 s slower than under rc.2, the UDP part 3 to 4 s quicker), changes 26 s apart up to 33 s (rc.2: 44 s), changes 3 s apart 50 to 55 s. No request failed meanwhile.
- **A planned failover loses 0.7 to 0.8 s.** A dead master loses 3.2 to 3.6 s (rc.2).
- **A replace lost 0.7 s of TCP and TLS and 0.8 s of UDP.**
- **A new certificate was served 15.8 to 21 s after its change** (rc.2), on a pair and from a Service's Secret. On a single balancer, under rc.1, 7.9 s.
- **A reboot of a single balancer leaves it without an answer for about 56 s** (rc.1). A reboot of a pair's master lost 0.8 s (rc.2).

### Open from the drill of rc.1 and the reviews of its fixes

- **A lone appliance of a pair sends one VRRP advert a second to its network's gateway.** It is addressed to `127.0.0.1` and reaches no other host. A gateway that logs such packets may log one a second while a pair has one appliance.
- **A second appliance that joins can hold the addresses beside the first for one pass**, when the push that tells the first of its peer fails in the pass where the new one starts.
- **A change made while an appliance joins or is replaced reaches the appliance that holds the address first**, not the standby.
- **A lone appliance whose public network was taken from the project stays `BUILD`**, and then reads the general "VMs did not become ready".
- **Why a cluster's second appliance was not built is in the API log only.** A tenant's own pair is still taken back as a whole when its second appliance cannot be built.
- **A reload that keeps HAProxy's master socket closed for more than 0.6 s is still one failed run of keepalived's track script.** Two in a row move the addresses.
- **A support file of keepalived that is edited by hand is not seen by a pass.** `keepalived.conf`, `haproxy.cfg`, the UDP files and the certificate files are.
- **While an appliance holds a change it refused, its certificate files are not checked.** The next push it takes writes them.
- **A single balancer whose heal cannot be pushed reads `DOWN`** with the file's message, although HAProxy still serves from memory.
- **Only the last `X-Forwarded-For` value is the balancer's.** A client's own value stays in front of it. Use the PROXY protocol for an address a client cannot forge.
- **A Service can be refused `QuotaExceeded` for a minute or two after other Services gave their addresses back.** It gets its address by itself. Seen on the lab under rc.1.
- **A tenant's cluster row names the old chart version until the next services sync** after the cluster's update. Seen on the lab under rc.1.

### Still true from v0.11.0

- **A member new to a config starts as up.** A node that is Ready and does not forward yet gets connections until its first check fails. Each waits the connect timeout, 2 s, and is then answered by another member.
- **One pair per cluster**, shared by all its Services. An in-cluster client of a Service with `ipMode: VIP` never passes the pair. The built-in quota is five public addresses.
- **The other known issues** in the [notes of v0.11.0](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.0) still hold, except "UDP is refused", "no TLS termination" and the lost requests at a reload, which this candidate answers by its code.

### Riding along

- **The sandbox invoice line.** A bill with sandbox items that round to 0.00 gets one summed item from now on (see "Billing"). No sent bill changes.
- **Sandbox platform code, switched off.** It cannot be turned on in this candidate: no BOM names its chart.
