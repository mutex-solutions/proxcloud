ProxCloud v0.11.1-rc.5 is the fifth release candidate of `v0.11.1`, published as a prerelease. `v0.11.1` completes layer 4 of the load balancer: UDP, TLS termination with certificates, and the layer 4 options, for a tenant's own balancers and for the Services of a managed Kubernetes cluster. The lab drill of [v0.11.1-rc.4](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.1-rc.4) ran every part of its plan: the upgrade from rc.3 did what its notes said, the two fixes of rc.4 held in every case it tried, and it found no defect. The tests of the release then found one, in a case the drill had tried once: a UDP client that keeps sending while the UDP service of an appliance stops and starts can stay unanswered for as long as it sends. rc.1 to rc.4 all have it. This candidate holds the fix. Nothing of the fix has run on a lab yet. It is not for production. `v0.11.0` stays the current stable release and `latest`.

The BOM (bill of materials) is the list of exact versions a managed-services platform runs. The BOM of this candidate is `0.11.1-rc.5`. Its pins are those of `0.11.1-rc.1`, `0.11.1-rc.2`, `0.11.1-rc.3` and `0.11.1-rc.4`. Against `0.11.0` it moves one thing: the cluster chart for new tenant clusters, `proxmox-kamaji-cluster` `0.1.17` → `0.1.19`.

## Highlights

- **Billing: from `v0.11.0`, rc.1 or rc.2, read the first part of "Upgrade" before you upgrade.** A public address of a Kubernetes Service, or one a cluster keeps, that has no floating IP row gets one at the first pass after the upgrade. It is billed from the time the address was allocated, not from the upgrade. A site that runs rc.3 or rc.4 has these rows already.
- **A UDP client is no longer left unanswered when the UDP service of an appliance stops and starts.** Under rc.1 to rc.4 a client that kept sending from one source port through such a stop and start could get no answer afterwards, for as long as it kept sending. The product makes such a stop and start by itself when a balancer's last UDP listener is removed and one is added again. See "Fixed since rc.4".
- **The notes of rc.4 were wrong about a restart of `nftables.service`.** They said that a client that sends through it is answered once the servers are back. Under rc.4 such a client was caught about 1 time in 8 at 5 datagrams a second, and 3 times in 4 at 50. The lab had tried it once. See "Fixed since rc.4".
- **From rc.4, the upgrade pushes one thing.** A pair with a UDP listener gets one file, the script its UDP service runs at a start and at a reload, with one reload of that service per appliance, the standby first. Every other balancer gets nothing.
- **The upgrade frees no client.** A UDP client that is unanswered before the upgrade stays so until it is silent for more than 30 s or sends from another source port. See "From `v0.11.1-rc.4`" in step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **The drill finding of rc.3 is fixed, and its fix held on the lab under rc.4.** A UDP client that already sends to an address and port when the listener for that port is added is answered. The [notes of v0.11.1-rc.4](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.1-rc.4) describe it.
- **An appliance the platform started reads up once it serves again.** After a failover the first health read takes the mark back. Seen on the lab under rc.4.
- **From rc.3, a pair with a UDP listener gets two files**: the nftables table of its UDP service and the script, with one reload of that service per appliance, the standby first. Every other balancer gets nothing.
- **A UDP port without a listener is silent since rc.4.** On a pair with a UDP listener, a datagram for a port that has no UDP listener gets no ICMP "port unreachable". See "From `v0.11.1-rc.3`" in step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **The three drill findings of rc.2 are fixed, and their fixes held on the lab under rc.3.** A change no longer waits while the image's package manager runs on an appliance. A changed connection limit of a UDP listener reaches the kernel. An address annotated to be kept and deleted within a second is kept. The [notes of v0.11.1-rc.3](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.1-rc.3) describe them.
- **From rc.2, a pair with a UDP listener is pushed once.** It gets its UDP config, one new drop-in, the table and the script, with one reload of the UDP service per appliance, the standby first. A limit that was stale is put right by that push. Every other balancer gets nothing.
- **Two of the fixes of rc.3 are in the cluster's controller.** A cluster keeps the controller it has. One that is on chart `0.1.19` under rc.1 or rc.2 is not offered an update by the tenant's console: see step 5 of "Upgrade". Nothing the controller does changed since rc.3.
- **Five listener protocols.** A listener is `tcp`, `udp`, `tls`, `http` or `https`. `v0.11.0` served `tcp` and `http` and refused the rest.
- **UDP, on a pair.** A highly available load balancer takes UDP listeners. The kernel's IPVS (LVS) balances the datagrams on the appliance that holds the address, next to HAProxy. A TCP and a UDP listener may share a port number. A balancer on one VM refuses UDP.
- **TLS ends on the balancer.** A project uploads certificates with their keys. A `tls` listener passes TCP to the servers, an `https` listener passes HTTP. One listener presents up to ten certificates and picks by the server name the client asks for (SNI). TLS 1.2 and 1.3 only, with a fixed list of suites.
- **The layer 4 options.** Session persistence by source address, PROXY protocol version 1 or 2, health checks of type `tcp`, `http`, `https`, `tls-hello` and `none` with their own interval, timeout, rise and fall, three timeouts, and a connection limit per listener.
- **Kubernetes Services get the same.** A Service of a managed cluster can have UDP ports, TCP and UDP on one address, session affinity, TLS from a `kubernetes.io/tls` Secret, and a set of `proxcloud.io/lb-*` annotations. A Service can also leave its public address to its cluster and take it again later.
- **The controller watches.** With cluster chart `0.1.19` a change of a Service or a node is sent to the portal about a second after it happens. Before, the controller listed the cluster every 10 s.
- **An existing cluster gets none of this by the portal's upgrade.** It keeps the controller it was made with until it is moved to chart `0.1.19`. Until then its UDP Services stay refused, so nothing gets an address and a bill without its owner's action. See step 5 of "Upgrade".
- **From `v0.11.0`, every load balancer is reloaded once after the upgrade.** The HAProxy config changed for every balancer, so each one is pushed once, the standby first. See step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **From rc.1, keepalived on a pair is reloaded once more.** The HAProxy config is the same to the byte. See step 2 of "Upgrade".
- **No request is lost at a reload or a node change any more** (#430). `v0.11.0` listed this as a known issue. On the lab a client loop counted 31365 requests and 0 failed across the upgrade to rc.1, 24627 and 0 failed across the upgrade from rc.1 to rc.2, and eight loops counted 183773 requests and 0 failed in the 47 minutes around the upgrade from rc.2 to rc.3, and six loops counted 35710 requests and 0 failed in the 15 minutes around the upgrade from rc.3 to rc.4. A node change under load was not run there.
- **Only `proxcloud.io/lb-*` annotations are read.** The product defines its own names. A manifest written for another cloud's controller must be renamed first: see Known issues.
- **What ran on a lab ran on rc.1, on rc.2, on rc.3 and on rc.4.** "Verified on a lab" names the candidate of each part. Nothing of this candidate's own fix has run on a lab.

## Fixed since rc.4

One pull request (#477). The tests on the release pull request of `v0.11.1` found the defect: the real-guest test of rc.4's UDP fix failed its step "nftables.service restarted" about one run in twenty.

- **A UDP client that kept sending while the UDP service stopped and started could stay unanswered.** On a pair, the UDP service is `proxcloud-lvs.service` on each appliance. A client that sends from one source port, and kept sending while that service stopped and started on the appliance that holds the address, could get no answer afterwards, for as long as it kept sending. The balancer read `ACTIVE`, the members `UP`, and a new client was answered, so nothing showed it. `v0.11.0` has no UDP.
- **Which events caught a client, under rc.1 to rc.4.** Every stop of the UDP service that a start follows within 30 s, on the appliance that holds the address:
  - *By the product itself:* a balancer's last UDP listener removed and a UDP listener added again while a client sends. On a cluster that is its only Service with a UDP port deleted and made again.
  - *By the product itself:* the keepalived process of the UDP service dying. systemd answers with a stop and a start.
  - *By hand:* `systemctl restart nftables` or `systemctl restart proxcloud-lvs`.
  - *By hand, new clients only:* `systemctl reload nftables` caught a client whose first datagrams arrived in those milliseconds, and never one that was already answered.
  - *Rarely, a start alone:* the first UDP listener on an address a client already sends to.
- **What did not catch a client.** A change that keeps a UDP listener on the balancer: a listener or a server added, changed or removed beside another UDP listener. Such a change reloads the service, which loads its table in one step. The address arriving on an appliance did not either. A real failover between two appliances and a boot are reasoned, not run.
- **How often, under rc.4.** Measured in real guests of both appliance images, not on a lab, with clients that each send from one socket. Per stop and start:
  - *A restart of `nftables.service`:* about 1 client in 8 was caught at 5 datagrams a second, and 3 in 4 at 50 (14 and 13 of 100 clients, 72 and 78 of 100).
  - *The last UDP listener removed and one added again*, 200 times per image: 51 and 49 of 800 clients at 5 a second, about 1 in 16, and 507 and 504 of 800 at 50 a second, about 5 in 8.
  - *The service's keepalived killed:* 15 of 15 and 11 of 15 such restarts left a client caught.
  - *A start alone:* about 1 client in 800 at 5 a second, and 1 in 100 at 50.
  - *Counted again* by the fix's own runs and by its review, 100 to 200 times per image for each of the first three events: between 1 client in 27 and 1 in 11 was caught at 5 a second, and between half and seven tenths at 50.
- **rc.1 to rc.3 caught such a client by the wider defect that rc.4 fixed.** They lack the chain that rc.4 added, so a client that sent while its port had no listener was caught. On the lab under rc.3, 4933 datagrams went unanswered after a restart of `nftables.service`. rc.4 closed that and left the moment described under "Why".
- **A client that is caught stays caught** until it is silent for more than 30 s, or sends from a new source port. It is not answered while it keeps sending. In real guests a caught client was still unanswered after 20 s of silence and answered after 33 s. That holds on every candidate, this one included, for a client that was caught before the fix was in place.
- **Why.** When the UDP service stops, it empties the balancer's table and then deletes its nftables table, the chain of rc.4 with it. The kernel goes on remembering flows for about 12 ms after the table is gone. A datagram that arrives in that moment is remembered without the address rewrite the UDP path needs. Once the service is back, every later datagram of that client keeps the entry alive, the server gets the client's own address as the source, and its answer leaves by the tenant's gateway and is lost. A second, far shorter moment sits inside the load of the table at a start.
- **Now.** At a start that finds no table loaded, the service's script empties the appliance's connection table once, right after the table is loaded and before forwarding is switched on. From the load on no such entry can be made: the chain of rc.4 drops what the balancer does not take, and what it takes is rewritten. So the flush leaves none. A reload finds the table and empties nothing. No package is added: the script sends the kernel one message with `perl`, which both appliance images have.
- **Measured with the fix, in real guests, not on a lab.** 200 stops and starts per event and image, with four clients at 5 datagrams a second and four at 50: 0 of 800 clients caught at either rate, for the listener removed and added again, for keepalived killed, and for a restart of `nftables.service`. The fix's review ran 100 more of each per image, and of a start alone: 0 caught. New clients during a restart of `nftables.service`: 0 of 37726 and 0 of 38359 sockets, where the script of rc.4 left 733 of 37773 and 635 of 38198. During a reload of it: 0 of 26664 and 0 of 27195, where the script of rc.4 left 166 of 26643 and 146 of 27117.
- **What a start costs now.** The whole connection table of the appliance is emptied, once, at each start of the UDP service that finds no table: a boot, the first UDP listener, a restart of the service or of `nftables.service`, and a reload of `nftables.service`. TCP is not tracked while the table is loaded, so HAProxy's connections do not notice. An answer that is on its way to a UDP question the appliance itself asked in that instant, such as a name lookup, is dropped once. In the review, over 60 restarts per image, none of 6549 and 4848 name lookups failed and one took 5 s, and none of 2866 and 2768 runs of `apt-get update` failed.
- **The journal says which way it went.** Each such start writes one line to the journal of `proxcloud-lvs.service`: "the nftables table was not loaded before: the connection table was emptied". When the flush fails, the service starts all the same and serves as rc.4 did, and the line says "the connection table could not be emptied". Nothing else shows a failed flush: see Known issues.
- **What it does not do.** It frees no client at the upgrade. It does not shorten the stop and start itself: UDP is unanswered on that appliance meanwhile, about 1.2 s at a restart in real guests.

## Upgrade

Use a lab or another non-production site. Production sites should stay on `v0.11.0` and wait for `v0.11.1`. These steps upgrade from `v0.11.0`, from `v0.11.1-rc.1`, from `v0.11.1-rc.2`, from `v0.11.1-rc.3` or from `v0.11.1-rc.4`. From an older release, read the [notes of v0.11.0](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.0) first: their steps and cautions still apply. The figures below were measured on one lab under rc.1, rc.2, rc.3 or rc.4, as named. Nothing was measured on this candidate.

**Billing first. This holds from `v0.11.0`, from rc.1 and from rc.2. A site that runs rc.3 or rc.4 has it behind it: its passes wrote these rows.**

- **Addresses that have no floating IP row get one, billed from their allocation time.** The floating IP row (`FLOATING_IP`) of a public address is what lists the address for the tenant and what bills it. Until rc.3 only the services sync (the changelog calls it the provider sync) wrote the row of an address that a cluster's balancer holds for a Service, or that a cluster keeps. Since rc.3 the balancer's own pass writes it.
- **When.** At the first pass of each cluster's balancer after the portal's upgrade: its controller's next request, within 30 s, or the services sync. Nobody has to act, and no plan row announces it.
- **From when it bills.** The row carries the time the address was allocated, not the time of the upgrade. Its bill item is counted from that time, or from the start of the open bill's cycle where the address is older than that. With a price by the hour, an address allocated ten days ago in this cycle is billed for those ten days at the next charge run.
- **It is the row a services sync writes.** A sync before the upgrade writes the same rows with the same start. The upgrade bills nothing a sync would not have billed. It bills it at the first pass, and without a sync.
- **Which sites this changes.** On a site whose services sync runs on schedule, at most the addresses taken since its last run. On a site that runs no sync by itself, such as a lab with the scheduler off, every address of a Service and every kept address that no sync has seen: they counted in the quota and were neither listed nor billed. On the lab under rc.2, 12 addresses were in use with 4 rows until a sync ran.
- **What it does not touch.** A tenant's own balancers and the public addresses of servers: their rows are the sync's, as before. No address moves, and nothing is pushed to an appliance for this.
- **What to check before the upgrade, on rc.2.** For each project with a cluster, read `GET /api/v1/project/{id}/quota-usage`. `network.addresses` lists every public address the quota counts, with `since` and `kind`. Compare the rows of kind `service` and `kept` with the project's floating IPs. Each one that has no floating IP is billed from its `since` after the upgrade.
- **What to check before the upgrade, on `v0.11.0` or rc.1.** They have no such list. Run a services sync and read the project's floating IPs and its open bill: the sync writes the rows the upgrade would write.
- **There is no switch for it.** An address that is given back before the upgrade gets no row: its Service deleted, or the kept address released on the cluster's page. An address that stays is billed from its allocation, by this pass or by the next sync.
- **On the lab, at the upgrade from rc.2 to rc.3,** six addresses got their floating IP row with the allocation time as its start. What a charge run then bills was read in the code only: the rows were read, and no charge run was made.

1. **Before the upgrade, look at six things.**

   - **The floating IP rows**, above.
   - **The platform's BOM.** The move is planned from the BOMs `0.10.1`, `0.10.2`, `0.11.0`, their candidates, `0.11.1-rc.1`, `0.11.1-rc.2`, `0.11.1-rc.3` and `0.11.1-rc.4`. A platform on an older BOM (`0.8.0` to `0.10.0` and their candidates, all on Kubernetes 1.35.10) is refused by this build. Move it to `0.10.1` first with the `v0.10.1` build, both parts, as the [notes of v0.10.1](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.10.1) describe.
   - **Services that carry `proxcloud.io/lb-internal`**, from `v0.11.0`. `v0.11.0` did not read this annotation. From this release a Service gets a public address only when the annotation is absent or false. A Service with `"true"`, `"yes"` or an empty value loses its address when its cluster gets the new controller. Set it to `"false"` or remove it where the address must stay. On the lab, under rc.1, two such Services lost their address within half a minute of their cluster's update.
   - **UDP listeners whose connection limit was changed under rc.1 or rc.2.** The kernel still holds the figure of before. The push of step 2 sets the figure the listener shows: a limit that never limited starts to refuse clients above it, and a removed one stops refusing.
   - **Whatever waits for ICMP "port unreachable" from a balancer's address.** After its push, a pair with a UDP listener answers nothing for a UDP port that has no listener. A monitor that reads that answer, or a UDP traceroute to the address, times out instead. A pair under rc.4 does so already.
   - **UDP clients that get no answer now.** The upgrade frees none of them. See the table in step 8.

2. **Upgrade the portal.** On the Docker Compose host, check out the tag and run the upgrade. The script takes its backup first. `compose.yaml` did not change since `v0.11.0`. See ["Upgrade" in the Compose guide](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.5/deploy/compose/README.md#upgrade).

   ```sh
   git fetch --tags && git checkout v0.11.1-rc.5
   ./proxcloud-up.sh upgrade v0.11.1-rc.5
   ```

   **From `v0.11.0`: every load balancer is pushed once.**

   - **Why.** The config the platform renders for an existing balancer is no longer the one `v0.11.0` rendered: it names a state file, tries a refused connect on another member, waits 2 s for a connect where it waited 5 s, and names its backends by pool. The files of a pair's keepalived changed too. So each balancer's recorded hash differs, and its next pass pushes it.
   - **What a push is.** One HAProxy reload per appliance, and one keepalived reload per appliance of a pair. A reload is not a restart: open connections are kept.
   - **The standby first.** A pair's standby gets the config before the appliance that holds the address. A config that HAProxy on the standby does not take is not sent to the other one. On each appliance the apply checks the file, reloads, proves that HAProxy listens on every address of it, and puts the old file back otherwise.
   - **What it costs once.** This reload is the last one that starts every member as up. A balancer with a dead member, or a Service with `externalTrafficPolicy: Local`, sees the known 2 to 4 seconds of lost requests once more.
   - **Until its push**, the health view of a balancer lists its members as `NOT LOADED`. The listeners are not affected.
   - **A balancer without a UDP or TLS listener gets no new package and one new file**, the state file.
   - **sshd is masked by that push**, on every appliance.
   - **No balancer of `v0.11.0` has a UDP listener**, so nothing of "From `v0.11.1-rc.2`", "From `v0.11.1-rc.3`" and "From `v0.11.1-rc.4`" below applies to it.
   - **This ran on the lab under rc.1**, as described: see "Verified on a lab". This candidate renders the same HAProxy config and the same keepalived files as rc.2, rc.3 and rc.4.

   **From `v0.11.1-rc.1`: a pair gets keepalived's files once more, and what follows for rc.2, rc.3 and rc.4.**

   - **Why.** Two files of a pair changed between rc.1 and rc.2: keepalived's track script and its notify script. `keepalived.conf` is the same to the byte.
   - **What happens.** The first pass writes keepalived's five files to each appliance of a pair and reloads keepalived once per appliance, the standby first. A reload keeps keepalived's state.
   - **HAProxy is not touched.** `haproxy.cfg` is the same to the byte as under rc.1, so it is not pushed and HAProxy is not reloaded.
   - **A single balancer gets nothing.** It runs no keepalived.
   - **A pair degraded by the rc.1 defect becomes `ACTIVE` at that first pass.** Its cluster's pending Services become ready.
   - **A pair with a UDP listener also gets the push described for rc.2**, in the same pass, with the table of rc.3's part and the script of rc.4's part in it.
   - **This ran on the lab, from rc.1 to rc.2**: see "Verified on a lab". From rc.1 to this candidate it was read from the code and is held by tests.

   **From `v0.11.1-rc.2`: a pair with a UDP listener gets its UDP config, a drop-in, the table and the script once. Nothing else is pushed.**

   - **A balancer without a UDP listener gets nothing.** That is a tenant's pair, a balancer on one VM, and a cluster's pair with TCP Services only. No file is written and nothing is reloaded. Its HAProxy config, `keepalived.conf` and keepalived's support files are the same to the byte as under rc.2.
   - **A pair with a UDP listener is pushed once**, a tenant's or a cluster's, at its first pass after the upgrade, the standby first and then the appliance that holds the address.
   - **What is written.** `/etc/keepalived/lvs.conf`, which gains one line, `warmup 0`, in every virtual server that has a health check. A new drop-in, `/etc/systemd/system/proxcloud-lvs.service.d/nftables.conf`, which ties the UDP service to `nftables.service`. And `/etc/proxcloud-lb/lvs.nft`, the table, which gains the chain `lb-input`: see "From `v0.11.1-rc.3`" below for what it does. And `/usr/local/sbin/proxcloud-lvs-net`, the script the service runs before it starts and at a reload: see "From `v0.11.1-rc.4`" below. The other two files of the UDP service, `lvs.sysctl` and the unit, are the same and are not written. A pair whose UDP pools all have the check switched off gets the drop-in, the table and the script.
   - **What is reloaded.** systemd's unit files, for the drop-in, and the UDP service `proxcloud-lvs.service`, once per appliance. The unit is not restarted. HAProxy and the keepalived that moves the address are not touched, so no address moves.
   - **What the reload does to traffic.** The service loads its nftables table again in one transaction, and keepalived's check process reads the config again. Members that are up stay in the kernel's table through it. On the lab, from rc.2 to rc.3, no client loop lost a request across this push, on four appliances. The table's file and the script were the same then. With the new table the reload ran on the lab from rc.3 to rc.4: see "From `v0.11.1-rc.3`". With the new script it ran in real guests only: see "From `v0.11.1-rc.4`".
   - **A stale connection limit is put right by that push.** Each member gets the limit its listener shows. A listener whose limit never changed is not edited.
   - **When the standby cannot take the push**, the balancer stays `ACTIVE` and says "A change waits", the appliance that holds the address is not pushed before the standby, and the platform tries again by itself.
   - **sshd.** The push masks it on the appliances it reaches. An appliance that gets no push keeps what it has until its next change: on the Ubuntu image sshd listens again after a reboot until the next HAProxy reload, behind the VM firewall, which drops the port.
   - **The package-manager fix pushes no file.** It changed the commands the portal sends, not a file on an appliance. An appliance meets them at its next push.
   - **The balancer's row gets two fields**, written without a push: `heldSince` while a change waits, and `served` on each Service of a cluster's balancer.
   - **This ran on the lab, from rc.2 to rc.3**, without the table's file and the script, which did not change then: see "Verified on a lab". From rc.2 to this candidate it was read from the code and is held by tests.

   **From `v0.11.1-rc.3`: a pair with a UDP listener gets two files and one reload of its UDP service. Nothing else is pushed.**

   - **A balancer without a UDP listener gets nothing.** That is a tenant's pair, a balancer on one VM, and a cluster's pair with TCP Services only. No file is written and nothing is reloaded.
   - **A pair with a UDP listener is pushed once**, a tenant's or a cluster's, at its first pass after the upgrade, the standby first and then the appliance that holds the address. Until that pass an appliance serves as it did before the upgrade.
   - **What is written.** `/etc/proxcloud-lb/lvs.nft`, the nftables table, which gains the chain `lb-input`. And `/usr/local/sbin/proxcloud-lvs-net`, the script: see "From `v0.11.1-rc.4`" below. The other four files of the UDP service are the same to the byte and are not written: `lvs.conf`, `lvs.sysctl`, the unit and its drop-in.
   - **What is reloaded.** The UDP service `proxcloud-lvs.service`, once per appliance. The unit is not restarted, systemd's unit files are not reloaded and no package is installed. HAProxy and the keepalived that moves the address are not touched, so no address moves.
   - **What the reload does to traffic.** The service loads the table again in one transaction, the new one in place of the old: there is no moment without the table or without its forward chain's drop. keepalived's check process reads its config again, and members that are up stay in the kernel's table. On the lab, from rc.3 to rc.4, four running one-socket flows lost none of 4464 datagrams each when the table was replaced. The script was the same then.
   - **One WARN line per appliance.** The operator's log has one WARN line per appliance for this push, "load balancer: an appliance's UDP table is not as its configuration has it; pushing it again". It is the state read finding the table of rc.3. The table of rc.1 and of rc.2 lacks the chain too, so an upgrade from them writes the same line: read in the code.
   - **On the wire, from that reload on.** A UDP datagram to an address of such a balancer for a port that has no UDP listener, or whose listener's pool has no server at all, is dropped. The client gets no ICMP "port unreachable" for it any more, and a UDP traceroute to the address ends without its last hop. A listener whose servers are all down answers "port unreachable" as before. TCP and ping are as before.
   - **A client that is caught at that moment stays caught.** The chain keeps a caught flow from being made and frees none, and the upgrade's reload empties nothing. A client that was already unanswered before the upgrade is answered once it was silent for more than 30 s, or sends from another source port.
   - **The health fix pushes no file and reloads nothing.** A pair whose row still reads `DEGRADED` with "its VM is stopped; starting it" or "it does not answer" for an appliance that is back is put right by the first health read of it, or by its next pass, whichever comes first. When the balancer was changed while that appliance was down, by the pass alone. ### Load balancers

- **A protocol per listener and the layer 4 options** (#431). Per listener: `protocol`, `connectionLimit` (1 to 20000) and `timeoutClientData`. Per pool: `sessionPersistence` by source address, `proxyVersion` 1 or 2, `timeoutMemberConnect` and `timeoutMemberData`. Per health check: the types `tcp`, `http`, `https`, `tls-hello` and `none`, and `interval`, `timeout`, `rise` and `fall`. A field at its default writes no line. See [the load balancer section of the cloud integration guide](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.5/docs/cloud-integration.md#load-balancers-haproxy-appliance).
- **UDP listeners on a pair** (#433). IPVS in NAT mode balances the datagrams. A second keepalived, in a unit of its own, fills its table and runs only health checks. The keepalived that moves the address is not touched: a server that comes or goes reloads the UDP service alone. Round robin, least connections or source address, with weights; persistence by source address; source ranges.
- **UDP health checks** (#433). `udp-connect` is the default: a datagram to the port and a ping. It takes an optional `payload` and `requireReply`. `tcp` or `http` on a port of your choice, or `none`. A server leaves after 2 to 10 failed checks.
- **The appliance is no router** (#433). Forwarding is on only while an nftables chain with policy drop is loaded that lets through nothing but the answers of balanced flows. Since rc.4 the same table drops a UDP datagram for a port without a listener (#473).
- **The UDP service empties the connection table at a start that found no table** (#477). See "Fixed since rc.4".
- **TLS and HTTPS listeners, and a project's certificates** (#434). Upload a PEM chain and its key, read what a certificate says, delete one no listener names. RSA of 2048 to 8192 bits or ECDSA P-256 or P-384, no passphrase, a chain of five at most. There is no update: a replacement is a new certificate named on the listeners in the old one's place. A change of certificate is one reload and closes no connection.
- **`https`** offers HTTP/2 and HTTP/1.1, speaks HTTP/1.1 to the servers and sets `X-Forwarded-For` and `X-Forwarded-Proto: https`. TLS to the servers is not offered.
- **The files of an appliance are compared at every pass** (#433). An appliance whose `haproxy.cfg`, `keepalived.conf` or UDP file is not the one the platform recorded is pushed again. Before, a file changed by hand stayed.
- **No request is lost at a reload or when a node changes** (#430). A reload keeps the state of every member, backends are named by pool, the controller sends a node from its first Ready on, and a refused connect is tried on another member at once.
- **A Service's address is written when it is served** (#430). The controller wrote it as soon as it was allocated, minutes before the first answer.
- **Smaller fixes of the second drill of `v0.11.0`** (#430). A balancer's lock is a lease of one minute. A paused VM is a known state. A pair with a paused appliance names the pause. An appliance whose HAProxy has no listeners gives up the address. Three messages are corrected.
- **The console** has the protocol list, the options, the UDP checks with their caveat, and a Certificates page.

### Managed Kubernetes

- **Gateway protocol 2** (#435). It only adds to protocol 1: a Service's annotations, affinity and chosen nodes in the request, its `ipMode` and Events in the answer. A controller of `v0.11.0` is served as before.
- **UDP and mixed Services** (#435). A Service's UDP ports are served by the pair's LVS. A TCP and a UDP port of one number share the Service's address. An SCTP port refuses the Service with `ProtocolNotSupported`.
- **Session affinity** (#435). `sessionAffinity: ClientIP` keeps a client on one node for the Service's timeout, for TCP and UDP.
- **TLS from a Service's Secret** (#438). `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` names one to four `kubernetes.io/tls` Secrets of the Service's namespace. The controller reads only the Secrets its Services name. A renewed Secret reaches both appliances as a new file and one reload. A Secret that is deleted or bad never takes TLS off a port and never costs a Service its address. See ["TLS from a Service's Secret"](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/blob/v0.11.1-rc.5/docs/managed-k8s.md#tls-from-a-services-secret).
- **Keeping an address** (#440). `proxcloud.io/lb-keep-address: "true"` leaves the Service's address to its cluster when the Service goes. A Service that names a kept address in `spec.loadBalancerIP` gets it. A kept address is billed and counts in the floating IP quota until it is released. A cluster keeps 5 at most.
- **The controller watches** (#439). It watches the Services, the Nodes and the TLS Secrets its Services name, and takes its turn one second after a change. The watch is only a trigger: a turn still lists everything and sends the whole state. An unchanged state is sent every 30 s, where it was every 10 s.
- **Which nodes are behind a Service follows Kubernetes' own rule** (#435). The exclude label is read as a boolean, and a node with the cluster autoscaler's removal taint is taken out.
- **Four review findings closed** (#441). `proxcloud.io/lb-internal` with a value that is no boolean refuses the Service. An empty `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` asks for TLS and is held like a bad Secret. The second of two quick changes no longer waits about 11 s. The console holds the API's rule for "Down after" on a UDP pool.
- **Chart `0.1.19` for new clusters, and the pages** (#442). Both consoles show each port's protocol, the Secret and expiry of a certificate, the kept addresses, and which controller a cluster has.

## Known issues

### UDP

- **UDP only on a pair.** A balancer on one VM answers `400` "UDP needs a high-availability load balancer".
- **A UDP server never sees the client's address.** It sees the appliance's private address. UDP has no PROXY protocol.
- **The default UDP check has blind spots.** Without a `payload` it sends random bytes to the service. Without `requireReply`, a closed port on a running server is not seen while the port has traffic. A server whose firewall drops silently is never seen as down without a reply check.
- **The default UDP check needs ICMP.** It pings the server. A server that does not answer a ping from the appliance is taken out, even when its UDP port works.
- **After a failover the other appliance spreads UDP clients afresh.** The appliances do not sync their connections. Source persistence starts afresh too, for TCP as well, and after every change of the configuration.
- **The connection limit of a UDP listener counts per server**, not per listener.
- **A UDP port without a listener is silent.** On a pair with a UDP listener, a datagram for a port that has no UDP listener, or whose listener's pool has no server at all, gets no ICMP "port unreachable". A listener whose servers are all down answers it as before.

### TLS

- **Where private keys rest.** In the database, encrypted with the portal's encryption key. In `backups/`, where the database dump and a copy of `.env` with `ENCRYPTION_KEY` lie side by side: whoever reads that directory reads every tenant's private key. And in clear on each appliance's disk (root only), so in any Proxmox backup, snapshot, clone or replica of an appliance VM.
- **A Service's Secret rests the same way.** The portal seals it in a row of the cluster's balancer and writes it to both appliances.
- **Whoever may write a Service in a namespace can have any TLS Secret of that namespace served**, as with an Ingress.
- **No update of a certificate.** Rotation is: upload the new one, name it on the listeners, delete the old one.
- **The TLS policy is fixed.** No TLS 1.1, no RSA key exchange, no CBC suite. No TLS to the servers.
- **A certificate with a signature the appliance's HAProxy refuses** is taken by the upload and refused at the push. The last config stays in use and the row says why.
- **While a Secret is unusable its whole Service is frozen** as it was served, its node list included when it has a node selector.

### Kubernetes

- **A request for an internal balancer under another cloud's annotation name is not recognised.** Only `proxcloud.io/lb-internal` is read. A Service that asks for an internal balancer with OCCM's annotation alone gets a **public** address. Rename the annotation before you apply such a manifest.
- **Annotations of any other prefix do nothing**, are not sent to the portal and raise no Event. `proxcloud.io/lb-proxy-protocol: "true"` is version 2 here; write `v1` for version 1.
- **Chart `0.1.19` with a `v0.11.0` controller image does not run.** The chart starts the controller with `--watch=true`, and that image exits at it. The one way into this pair is a portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` that is then asked to enable the controller again, or to set the chart version, on a cluster that is on `0.1.19`. Do neither while the portal is rolled back. The Services of such a cluster stay as they were last served.
- **A portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` takes the address of a TLS Service** and gives it a new one after the roll forward. Each turn of a new controller is then two requests, and the old portal audits the refused one.
- **A portal rolled back to `v0.11.0` drops the list of kept addresses.** They stay allocated, billed and in the floating IP quota, and no page lists them, also after the roll forward, until the cluster is deleted. Release them before a rollback (step 7).
- **An existing cluster is not upgraded for you.** It keeps protocol 1 until its chart is moved (step 5). **Enable load balancer controller**, which also rotates the credential, moves it too: its refused UDP Services then get addresses without the tenant acting.
- **A cluster on the `v0.11.0` controller that adds `proxcloud.io/lb-tls-secret` serves the port plain**, with no Event: that controller sends no such annotation.
- **A cluster's state over 64 KiB is refused as a whole.** Nothing changes and every address stays; each Service gets a Warning Event `TooLarge`.
- **The gateway has no rate limit per cluster.** A Service that flaps causes about one call a second, where it was one per 10 s.
- **"The controller last reported" reads up to a minute and a half.** A degraded pair of a quiet cluster is looked at in steps of 30 s, where it was 10 s.
- **Pool names of a cluster's balancer changed.** Nothing reads them but people.

- **A lone appliance whose public network was taken from the project stays `BUILD`**, and then reads the general "VMs did not become ready".
- **Why a cluster's second appliance was not built is in the API log only.** A tenant's own pair is still taken back as a whole when its second appliance cannot be built.
- **A reload that keeps HAProxy's master socket closed for more than 0.6 s is still one failed run of keepalived's track script.** Two in a row move the addresses.
- **A support file of keepalived that is edited by hand is not seen by a pass.** `keepalived.conf`, `haproxy.cfg`, the UDP files and the certificate files are.
- **While an appliance holds a change it refused, its certificate files are not checked.** The next push it takes writes them.
- **A single balancer whose heal cannot be pushed reads `DOWN`** with the file's message, although HAProxy still serves from memory.
- **Only the last `X-Forwarded-For` value is the balancer's.** A client's own value stays in front of it. Use the PROXY protocol for an address a client cannot forge.
- **A Service can be refused `QuotaExceeded` for a minute or two after other Services gave their addresses back.** It gets its address by itself. Seen on the lab under rc.1.
- **A tenant's cluster row names the old chart version until the next services sync** after the cluster's update. Seen on the lab under rc.1.

### Still true from v0.11.0

- **A member new to a config starts as up.** A node that is Ready and does not forward yet gets connections until its first check fails. Each waits the connect timeout, 2 s, and is then answered by another member.
- **One pair per cluster**, shared by all its Services. An in-cluster client of a Service with `ipMode: VIP` never passes the pair. The built-in quota is five public addresses.
- **The other known issues** in the [notes of v0.11.0](https://github.com/mutex-solutions/proxcloud/releases/tag/v0.11.0) still hold, except "UDP is refused", "no TLS termination" and the lost requests at a reload, which this candidate answers by its code.

### Riding along

- **The sandbox invoice line.** A bill with sandbox items that round to 0.00 gets one summed item from now on (see "Billing"). No sent bill changes.
- **Sandbox platform code, switched off.** It cannot be turned on in this candidate: no BOM names its chart.
